# Other Asian Traditional Systems Audit — Bios from the Steppe, the Mekong, the Java Lowlands, and the Barangay

**Scope:** Bios viewed from the *smaller codified Asian traditional systems* — those that are neither Chinese TCM nor Ayurveda nor Sowa Rigpa nor Japanese Kampo, and that consequently rarely receive their own audit in software documentation. The reviewing panel: a Mongolian *anagaakh ukhaan* practitioner trained in the Manba Datsan curriculum (Ulaanbaatar) and a *bariach* bonesetter from Khovd; a Vietnamese *thầy thuốc Đông y* trained at the Hanoi National Hospital of Acupuncture, working both the Northern *Thuốc Bắc* and the Southern *Thuốc Nam* repertoires; a Thai *phaet phaen boran* registered under the Department for Development of Thai Traditional and Alternative Medicine, with practice spanning herbal pharmacy, *Nuad Thai* massage (UNESCO intangible heritage), and *phadung khan* midwifery; a *kru khmer* in rural Battambang province working in a tradition that lost most of its written transmission in 1975–79 and has been reconstructed substantially from oral and monastic sources; a Lao herbalist working out of a Vientiane-province district where Khmer, Thai, and Sino-Vietnamese influences mix on the same shelf; a Burmese practitioner registered under the Department of Traditional Medicine in Naypyidaw, with training across the four classical branches of *Desana*, *Bethitza*, *Netkhatta*, and *Vijjadhara*; an Indonesian *Jamu* practitioner — both an apothecary-shop *peracik* in Yogyakarta and a *jamu gendong* (the women who carry the daily jamu round on their backs in the morning streets); and a Filipino *manghihilot* working a rural barangay outside Iloilo whose lineage covers manual therapy, *paltik* bone-setting, and *pulso* pulse-reading.
**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Branch:** `feat/metric-info-sheets-on-read`
**Lens:** Other Asian traditional systems, *plural*. The eight practitioner perspectives above do not collapse into a single voice — a Burmese *Bethitza* purification specialist and a *jamu gendong* on her morning round work in registers as different as those of a hospital pharmacist and a market herbalist. Where they converge, this audit says so. Where they diverge, both readings are reported. Where Bios's biomedical frame is *orthogonal* to a practitioner's diagnostic frame, that is stated as orthogonality, not as a defect.
**Auditor:** Claude (Opus 4.7)

This audit does **not** ask Bios to become a *jamu* dispensary, a *Nuad Thai* clinic-management system, a *bariach* fracture-reduction protocol, a *Vijjadhara* alchemical workshop, or an Ifá-equivalent divinatory engine. It asks where, viewed through each panel member's frame, Bios's current observations are usable, where they are silent, where they are *structurally aligned* with a particular tradition's posture (sometimes in ways the manifesto may not have noticed), and where the differences are differences of *kind* that should be named rather than smoothed over.

## A note on overlap with prior audits, and on what is genuinely distinct

Three significant prior audits sit adjacent to this one. The *Mongolian* section will overlap substantially with [SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) — *anagaakh ukhaan* shares the *Gyud Zhi* curricular root with Tibetan medicine, and the three-*Nyepa* humoural frame, the *rgyud bzhi*-style pulse and urine examinations, the seasonal regimen, and the high-altitude physiology gap are the same gaps in a different language. The audit names the alignment and refuses to repeat the analysis — the Mongolian-specific contributions (the *bariach* bonesetting tradition, the bloodletting refinements, the nomadic-pastoralist context, *chimedeg* manual therapy, the modernisation of mobile clinics) are what get the new treatment.

The *Vietnamese* section overlaps substantially with [TCM_POV.md](TCM_POV.md) on its Northern (*Thuốc Bắc*) face — the eight-principles, zang-fu, six-pathogen, four-level frameworks all apply — but diverges on its Southern (*Thuốc Nam*) face into an indigenous Vietnamese pharmacopeia, on its integration-with-biomedicine posture (Đông y is fully integrated into the Vietnamese national health system in a way Chinese TCM is not in most other jurisdictions), on its tropical-disease catchment, on its specific manual therapies (*giác hơi* cupping and *cạo gió* coin-rubbing leave marks that biomedical clinicians regularly mistake for abuse — a real cross-cultural diagnostic confound), and on its urban Android adoption pattern.

The *Burmese* and *Thai* sections share Buddhist framing and a Pali/Sanskrit-influenced canonical vocabulary, but the institutional registers differ — Burmese DTM under a more centralised state structure, Thai *phaet phaen boran* under a kingdom-supported and tourism-integrated framework. The *Khmer*, *Lao*, *Jamu* and *Hilot* sections each get standalone treatment because they have not previously been audited and because each carries a distinct contribution: Khmer the cultural-loss-and-reconstruction problem, Lao the syncretic-influence problem, Jamu the regulated-pharmacy + daily-life-integration problem, Hilot the manual-therapy + Catholic-syncretic + family-network problem.

The audit also generalises a finding the [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) audit named first: **Bios's condition library is Northern-temperate-biased.** The same finding applies, with different specifics, across most of mainland and maritime Southeast Asia.

## Executive summary

Bios is, at the level of *architectural posture*, **substantially compatible with the institutional realities of the eight traditions on this panel** — and almost universally invisible to them at the level of *content*. The local-first storage, no-Google-Play-Services dependency, offline-first operation, and "the owner decides what leaves the device" architecture are precisely the technical commitments that fit the dominant deployment context across the panel: mid-tier Android (2018–2022 chipsets) on intermittently-connected mobile data, frequently used in clinics or marketplace settings with no cloud infrastructure, by practitioners whose patient population includes people for whom data-broker exposure is a real material risk. The panel notes that this alignment is structural, not performed. Bios did not build "for Southeast Asia" or "for the Mongolian steppe" — it built a privacy-first, owner-sovereign, low-spec-friendly architecture, and that architecture happens to be the one this catchment has been waiting for from consumer health software.

At the level of *content*, Bios's gaps reproduce the pattern the [African audit](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named: the 33+ condition patterns track Northern-temperate chronic-disease prevalence, the citation list is Western institutional, the region configs cover US/GB/EU/CA/AU/JP and silently fall back to EU for any unmapped country, and no language overlay exists for Mongolian (*Khalkha* in Cyrillic or traditional script), Vietnamese, Thai, Khmer, Lao, Burmese (*Myanmar bama*), Bahasa Indonesia, Javanese, Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilonggo, Hiligaynon, or any other language the panel uses with patients.

Beyond the implementation gaps, the panel converges on several **fundamental orthogonalities** the audit names honestly. The *Vijjadhara* (Burmese alchemical-mystical) branch and the *saiyasart* (Thai spiritual medicine) branch are not biomarker-visible by construction. The *kru khmer*'s ritual purification work and the *Hilot* practitioner's *pasubo* (offering) practices are not measurable phenomena; they were not constructed to be. The *jamu gendong*'s daily round is a relationship as much as a pharmacy transaction; Bios cannot model relationships. These are orthogonalities, not gaps, and the audit refuses to paper over them.

Ordered by impact within the things Bios *could* address without overreaching:

1. **No regional disease-pattern library for tropical / subtropical Southeast Asia, mainland or maritime.** The condition library in [ConditionPatterns.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) is Northern-temperate-anchored. There is no pattern for *dengue* (the biphasic saddleback fever-curve + plasma-leak warning signs + bleeding tendency staging WHO 2009 codifies), no pattern for *malaria-onset* (the [African audit §2.1](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) flagged this; it applies equally to the Greater Mekong sub-region where *P. vivax* and increasingly artemisinin-resistant *P. falciparum* dominate), no pattern for *typhoid* (step-ladder fever, Faget's sign), no pattern for *chikungunya* (acute polyarthralgia + biphasic fever), no pattern for *Japanese encephalitis* prodrome, no pattern for *leptospirosis*, no pattern for *melioidosis* (endemic across Northeast Thailand, Cambodia, Lao PDR, and northern Australia — the "Vietnamese time-bomb" of the surgical literature), no pattern for *scrub typhus*, no pattern for the staged *enterovirus 71 / hand-foot-and-mouth* paediatric presentations that periodically epidemic across the region. This is the single largest content gap relative to the disease landscape the panel actually sees.

2. **No region configs for any country represented on the panel.** [RegionConfigProvider.kt:199-206](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt#L199-L206) maps `US`, `GB`, `EU`, `CA`, `AU`, `JP`. There is no `MN` (Mongolia), `VN` (Vietnam), `TH` (Thailand), `KH` (Cambodia), `LA` (Lao PDR), `MM` (Myanmar / Burma), `ID` (Indonesia), `PH` (Philippines), `SG` (Singapore), `MY` (Malaysia), `BN` (Brunei), or `TL` (Timor-Leste). A device locale set to `vi_VN`, `th_TH`, `id_ID`, `tl_PH`, `mn_MN`, `my_MM`, `km_KH` or `lo_LA` therefore receives `euConfig()` via the fallback at [RegionConfigProvider.kt:215-218](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt#L215-L218), with EMA regulatory framing, "CE-marked medical device" disclaimer text that has no jurisdictional meaning, and ESC/ESH cardiovascular cutoffs that — while broadly close to the regional guidelines — are sourced wrong. The Vietnamese owner's prescriber would reference the Drug Administration of Vietnam (DAV); the Indonesian owner's would reference BPOM (and for traditional medicine specifically, BPOM's *Obat Tradisional* division); the Thai owner's would reference the FDA Thailand; the Mongolian owner's would reference the Mongolian Ministry of Health and the National Center for Traditional Medicine; the Burmese owner's would reference the Department of Food and Drug Administration under MoHS plus the Department of Traditional Medicine for traditional preparations; the Filipino owner's would reference FDA Philippines and the Philippine Institute of Traditional and Alternative Health Care (PITAHC).

3. **No surface for the panel's pharmacopeias.** The medication-annotation surface read by [AnomalyDetector.kt:34, 376-378](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/engine/AnomalyDetector.kt#L34) accepts free-text drug names. This is enough to *technically* store "the patient takes *Andrographis paniculata* decoction in the mornings" or "the patient is on *Curcuma xanthorrhiza* in a jamu preparation," but it offers no first-class structure for the things the panel actually prescribes. The Vietnamese *Thuốc Nam* pharmacy includes *Andrographis paniculata* (*xuyên tâm liên* — widely used during the 2021 COVID surge), *Centella asiatica* (*rau má*), *Eclipta prostrata* (*cỏ mực*), *Phyllanthus urinaria* (*chó đẻ răng cưa*) — none with RxNorm codes. The Thai pharmacopeia (Department for Development of Thai Traditional and Alternative Medicine's National List of Essential Herbal Medicines, expanded incrementally since 1999) includes *Andrographis* (*ฟ้าทะลายโจร, fa-talai-jone*), *Curcuma longa*, *Zingiber officinale*, *Tinospora crispa* (*boraphet*), *Garcinia atroviridis*, and several dozen others. The Burmese DTM formulary, the Khmer *thnam khmer* pharmacy, the Lao herbal pharmacy, the Indonesian *jamu* register under BPOM (which now formally distinguishes *Jamu*, *Obat Herbal Terstandar*, and *Fitofarmaka* tiers), and the Filipino DOH-approved herbals (DOH "10 Medicinal Plants" — *Lagundi*, *Sambong*, *Akapulko*, *Yerba Buena*, *Tsaang Gubat*, *Niyog-Niyogan*, *Bayabas*, *Bawang*, *Ulasimang Bato*, *Ampalaya*) are each finite, catalogued formularies that *could* be first-class in the annotation surface but currently are not.

4. **No surface for the manual-therapy and intervention-event vocabulary common across the panel.** Bios has no `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric. The traditions on this panel are *manual-therapy-heavy* in a way the East Asian audits already partially flagged but not exhaustively: Mongolian *chimedeg* manual therapy and *bariach* bonesetting; Vietnamese *giác hơi* cupping (which leaves circular marks for several days) and *cạo gió* coin-rubbing (which leaves linear erythematous striae) and Vietnamese bone-setting; Thai *Nuad Thai* (a structured 90- to 120-minute therapeutic massage, recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage in 2019) and Thai herbal-compress therapy (*luk pra kob*); Khmer manual-therapy practices including *thveu sa-aat* purification massage; Lao bone-setting; Burmese manipulative therapy; Indonesian *pijat* (massage) and *sangkal putung* (bone-setting); and Filipino *Hilot* manual therapy and *paltik* bone-setting. None of these have a place to live as timestamped intervention events in Bios's data model. An owner who receives a 90-minute *Nuad Thai* session on Tuesday afternoon has measurable HRV and skin-temperature responses for several hours afterward, and there is no anchor in the data to read the wearable trace against.

5. **The *giác hơi* and *cạo gió* cross-cultural diagnostic confound is unrecognised in the data model — and it could be.** This is specific to the Vietnamese (and to a lesser extent broader-East-Asian) practice but worth naming as an example of the more general problem. *Cạo gió* leaves linear erythematous-to-petechial marks on the back, neck, and shoulders that biomedical paediatricians and emergency physicians outside Vietnam (and outside Vietnamese diaspora communities in the US, France, and Australia) have repeatedly mistaken for child abuse — a confound documented since the 1980s in the paediatrics literature (Tan, *Pediatrics*; Yeatman; Look; Risser). *Giác hơi* cupping leaves circular ecchymotic marks that get the same misreading. An owner who is wearing Bios and presents to an ED in Sydney with their child showing *cạo gió* marks could materially benefit from the wearable record being explicit: "the owner annotated *cạo gió* applied at [timestamp], affected area [neck, shoulders, upper back] — the marks visible at the ED visit are consistent with this intervention, not with trauma." This is not Bios's job to *resolve* the confound, but giving the owner a place to annotate the intervention so the timeline carries the context is unambiguously within Bios's role.

6. **No language localisation into any panel-relevant language.** [strings.xml](../../android/app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml) has no `values-vi/` (Vietnamese, ~95 M speakers domestically plus diaspora), `values-th/` (Thai, ~70 M), `values-id/` (Bahasa Indonesia, ~280 M with Malaysian-Bahasa overlap), `values-tl/` or `values-fil/` (Filipino/Tagalog, ~80 M with English bilingualism but vernacular health-information access is still the policy goal), `values-my/` (Myanmar / Burmese, ~33 M with the Myanmar / Unicode-vs-Zawgyi script complication that is itself a real engineering problem), `values-km/` (Khmer, ~17 M), `values-lo/` (Lao, ~7 M), `values-mn/` (Mongolian — Cyrillic *Mongol Kiril* in MN, traditional vertical Mongol script in some Inner Mongolian contexts), `values-jv/` (Javanese, ~80 M), `values-su/` (Sundanese, ~40 M), `values-ceb/` (Cebuano), or `values-hil/` (Hiligaynon). The string surface is currently small but the alert-text surface in [ConditionPatterns.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) is *substantial* and hardcoded in English in `explanation`, `suggestedAction`, `earlyDetection`, `prevention`, `healing`, `risks` — six paragraphs per pattern, 33+ patterns — none of which is localisable in the current architecture. The same refactor [the African audit recommended](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) (move text fields out of the data class and into `strings.xml`) is the unblocking step for this catchment too.

7. **No high-altitude or extreme-cold modulation for the Mongolian steppe context.** The [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.1](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) raised altitude as the single most consequential gap for the Himalayan plateau. Mongolia is geographically distinct (most of the populated steppe sits between 1,200 and 1,700 m — not the Tibetan-plateau extreme) but adds *extreme cold* (winter lows of −30°C to −45°C across the central and northern aimags, daily temperature swings of 30–40°C between winter night and day) and *very-low-humidity continental dry-air* exposure. Both materially affect baselines an instrument like Bios reads — skin temperature, hydration-related HR/HRV signals, respiratory rate, sleep architecture (the cold-night/warm-ger contrast is sleep-relevant). Bios has no `EnvironmentalContext.climateBand` annotation. The [Sowa Rigpa audit's §2.6 recommendation](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) — an owner-set climate band with categorical fields — generalises directly to the Mongolian context and to the *tropical-monsoonal* and *tropical-equatorial* catchment of the rest of the panel (where humidity, heat-index, and monsoon-season-vs-dry-season modulation matter for baseline interpretation).

8. **Birth attendance by traditional midwife is still common across most of the panel's catchment, and the pregnancy-state surface has no concept of attendant.** The [African audit §2.6](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) raised the TBA-attendant problem; the same problem applies here, with named local equivalents: Thai *phadung khan*, Indonesian *dukun bayi*, Filipino *komadrona* / *hilot ng panganganak*, Vietnamese *bà mụ vườn* (rural traditional midwife) and *bà đỡ*, Burmese *let-thay* (traditional midwife — increasingly integrated into the formal cadre under the Department of Traditional Medicine and the Maternal and Child Health programme), Lao and Khmer traditional birth attendants. WHO's posture has shifted from "phase out" to "integrate into the referral chain" over the last fifteen years, and several countries on the panel (Indonesia notably, with the *Bidan Praktik Mandiri* + traditional birth attendant collaboration model) have explicit integration policies. [PhysiologyState](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/physiology/PhysiologyState.kt) has the three pregnancy trimesters and postpartum but no `attendantType` field and no concept of attendance.

9. **No surface for the four-classical-branches structure of Burmese traditional medicine — and this is *correct*, but worth naming.** The DTM under MoHS organises Burmese traditional medicine into four classical branches: *Desana* (theoretical / doctrinal medicine, *Vedasathay*-derived), *Bethitza* (purification / panchakarma-equivalent), *Netkhatta* (astrological medicine, time-and-direction-of-treatment determination), and *Vijjadhara* (alchemical / occult, including *aggi-rasa* mercury-and-mineral preparations under careful supervision). The first two map *partially* onto things Bios already does: *Desana*'s humoural-balance reasoning maps onto the same *Nyepa* / *Tridosha* / *Tridoṣa-equivalent* surfaces the [Sowa Rigpa](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [Ayurveda](AYURVEDA_POV.md) audits already recommended, and *Bethitza*'s purification regimens map onto the same intervention-event surface this audit recommends in (4) above. The latter two — *Netkhatta* (astrological treatment-timing) and *Vijjadhara* (alchemical practice) — are *orthogonal* to biomarker measurement in the same way the [African audit §3.2](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) called Ifá divination orthogonal. Bios should not attempt either. The audit names them not as gaps but as boundaries.

10. **The *kru khmer* tradition's reconstruction problem is invisible to the codebase, and asks something different of Bios.** Cambodian traditional medicine experienced catastrophic cultural-knowledge loss during 1975–79; the senior practitioners who held the *kbun srok srè* (village-and-forest pharmacy) traditions were specifically targeted, and the written texts (*satra*, palm-leaf manuscripts) held in temple libraries were largely destroyed. What survives is a partly-reconstructed tradition relying on oral transmission, monastic memory, and the work of organisations like the Cambodian Traditional Healers Association and the National Centre of Traditional Medicine in Phnom Penh, supplemented by ethnobotanical research projects (Ashwell, Walston) since the early 1990s. The panel's reading: Bios cannot help reconstruct lost knowledge, and the audit makes no such ask. What Bios *can* do is *not pretend completeness* — when the alert-text surface refers an owner to "your healthcare provider" in a Khmer locale, the practitioner the owner consults may be a *kru khmer* whose own diagnostic vocabulary is itself in active reconstruction. The audit recommends the same epistemological humility in the text the [African audit §3.3](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) recommended for spirit-and-ancestral etiology: Bios reports what it sees, and stops.

11. **The *Hilot* tradition's *pulso* pulse-reading is a real diagnostic axis that Bios's PPG layer could half-serve — and the [TCM audit §2.2](TCM_POV.md) and [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.3](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [Kampo audit §2.7](KAMPO_POV.md) findings on PPG-waveform-discard apply identically.** The *manghihilot* reads *pulso* in a less elaborated taxonomy than Chinese 28-qualities — typically four to six qualities are taught, with terms drawn from Tagalog and from Spanish-Latin-derived medical loanwords (the Catholic-syncretic + colonial layer is part of the tradition's structure) — but the *quality* of the pulse is what the practitioner reads, not the rate. The same discarded-PPG-morphology problem applies. The audit refers to the three prior East Asian audits rather than repeating the analysis.

12. **The *Nuad Thai* + *luk pra kob* + *Hilot* + *pijat* + *chimedeg* manual-therapy traditions could be served by an `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric, and there is currently nowhere clean for the data to land.** Generalised from (4) above. The post-session physiological trace (HRV elevation typically 60–120 minutes post-massage; skin temperature elevation in the worked area; sleep-quality improvement that night) is *measurable* in Bios's existing data streams, but there is no event anchor to align the trace against. The [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.7](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) raised the same issue for *me btsa'* moxibustion and *bsku mnye* oil massage; the same architectural answer applies.

The rest of this audit walks each tradition with its distinctive contributions, each gap with file references, and the orthogonalities the panel asks Bios to name and preserve.

These are not parity wins. Several of these are places where Bios is *meaningfully ahead* of the consumer-health category on dimensions that matter for this catchment — and where, the panel notes, the alignment is *structural* rather than performed. The panel asks that the alignment be named explicitly in the project's positioning, because alignments of this kind get lost when documentation focuses on individual-user privacy and not on collective deployment-context fit.

## 2. Tradition-specific findings

This section walks each tradition on the panel with its distinctive contribution, the gaps that are specific to that tradition's frame, and the alignments worth naming. Sections are deliberately uneven in length — the Mongolian section references the [Sowa Rigpa audit](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) extensively and is therefore short; the Khmer and Lao sections are shorter because the traditions themselves are less codified in available documentation and the audit refuses to overclaim coverage; the Vietnamese, Thai, Jamu, and Hilot sections are longer because each has distinctive contributions Bios could meaningfully serve.

### 2.1 Mongolian — *Anagaakh Ukhaan*

Mongolian traditional medicine (*anagaakh ukhaan*, "the science of healing") shares the *rgyud bzhi* (Four Tantras) curricular root with Sowa Rigpa, transmitted via the monastic Manba Datsan colleges from the seventeenth century onward, and reorganised under state framing post-1990. The three-*Nyepa* (*khii*, *shar*, *badgan* in Mongolian — wind, bile, phlegm) framework, the *rgyud bzhi*-style pulse and urine examinations, the seasonal regimen, and the high-altitude physiology gap are the same as those covered in [SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md). The audit names the overlap and refuses to repeat the analysis.

What the Mongolian section adds:

**Bonesetting (*bariach*) as a distinct codified specialty.** The *bariach* tradition is a manual-therapy lineage with explicit fracture-and-dislocation reduction techniques, internationally studied through the 1990s–2010s (Khishigsuren, ULAANBAATAR; collaborations with Mongolian University of Medical Sciences). The *bariach*-served owner — typically a herder with a fall-and-fracture history, often miles from a hospital — is exactly the post-reduction-monitoring-window case the [African audit §4.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) raised for Yoruba bonesetters. The same recommendation applies: a post-reduction monitoring template with `PAIN_SCORE` trajectory ([MetricType.kt:79](../../android/bios-contracts/src/main/kotlin/com/bios/contracts/MetricType.kt#L79)), distal-warmth proxy, HR/activity asymmetry, and an `URGENT` escalation path for the compartment-syndrome signature. The *bariach* would use Bios in the same instrument-not-replacement frame as the Yoruba bonesetter.

**Bloodletting (*khanakh*, *Tib. gtar ga*) refinements.** Mongolian bloodletting differs from the Tibetan tradition in specific named-point and seasonal conventions. The relevant Bios surface is the same `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric the [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.7](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) recommended; the only Mongolian-specific note is that the post-bloodletting acute haematocrit-and-volume shift produces measurable RHR and HRV deviations for 24–72 hours, and the practitioner would value an annotation anchor so the post-procedure trace reads as expected rather than as a `cardiovascular_stress` false-positive.

**Nomadic-pastoralist context.** A substantial fraction of Mongolian patients live in mobile *ger* (yurt) encampments that move with the seasonal herding pattern, far from fixed clinics. The *anagaakh ukhaan* tradition includes the *aimag*-and-soum-level mobile-clinic outreach model that the National Center for Traditional Medicine has been formalising since the 2000s. Bios's offline-first, no-cloud-required, low-power Android architecture is structurally appropriate for this deployment context in a way few consumer health apps are. The panel notes that the dual-script localisation problem (*Mongol Kiril* in Cyrillic for MN; traditional vertical *Mongol bichig* in some Inner Mongolian contexts and in the post-2020 revival in MN) is a real engineering complication that Android handles tolerably but that needs explicit attention in any `values-mn/` overlay.

**Extreme-cold modulation.** As noted in the executive summary §7, Mongolia adds extreme continental cold (winter lows of −30°C to −45°C across most aimags) to the catchment Bios should think about. Skin-temperature baselines, sleep-architecture-in-cold-ger patterns, hydration-related autonomic shifts, and respiratory-rate baselines in cold-air exposure all materially affect the engine's reads. The same `EnvironmentalContext.climateBand` annotation the [Sowa Rigpa audit](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) recommended covers this.

**Chimedeg (manual therapy).** Distinct from *bariach* (bonesetting); *chimedeg* is closer to soft-tissue manipulation, taught as a separate lineage. The intervention-event surface in (4) of the executive summary covers it.

### 2.2 Vietnamese — *Đông y* (*Thuốc Bắc* and *Thuốc Nam*)

Vietnamese traditional medicine occupies a distinctive institutional position on the panel: it is *fully integrated* into the Vietnamese national health system, with parallel hospital structures (the Hanoi National Hospital of Traditional Medicine, the National Hospital of Acupuncture, the Ho Chi Minh City hospitals of traditional medicine), a recognised medical specialty pathway (the *Bác sĩ Y học cổ truyền* — TM MD), and routine integration into combined biomedical-traditional treatment protocols. This is structurally closer to the Korean Hanui-hak and Chinese TCM institutional patterns than to the Thai or Khmer patterns, and the panel's Vietnamese member reads Bios as a *direct potential adjunct* to clinical practice in the same way the Kampo audit's reader does.

The Vietnamese tradition is conventionally split into two schools, and the distinction matters for Bios:

- **Northern school (*Thuốc Bắc*)** — Sino-derived materia medica and theoretical framework. The classical eight-principles, zang-fu pattern differentiation, six-pathogen / four-level infection staging, and pulse-and-tongue diagnostic framework apply directly. The [TCM audit](TCM_POV.md) findings on §2.1 eight principles, §2.2 pulse morphology discard, §2.3 tongue-photo journal absence, §2.4 zang-fu pattern library, §2.7 wei-qi-ying-xue infection staging, §2.10 meridian-time clock — all apply *as-is* to the *Thuốc Bắc* face of Vietnamese practice. The audit refers to those findings.
- **Southern school (*Thuốc Nam*)** — indigenous Vietnamese pharmacopeia, with a substantially different plant list, lower reliance on imported Chinese materia medica, and a distinct lineage rooted in the seventeenth-century work of Lê Hữu Trác (*Hải Thượng Lãn Ông*) and his ten-volume *Hải Thượng y tông tâm lĩnh*. This is the face of Vietnamese medicine that is *not* covered by the TCM audit and that needs distinctive attention here.

**Distinctive gaps for the *Thuốc Nam* face:**

- The pharmacopeia includes plants that have *no Chinese equivalent* and therefore no representation in any TCM-oriented herbal database. *Centella asiatica* (*rau má*), *Eclipta prostrata* (*cỏ mực*), *Phyllanthus urinaria* (*chó đẻ răng cưa*), *Andrographis paniculata* (*xuyên tâm liên* — usage spiked in early 2021 during the COVID Delta wave, when the Ministry of Health authorised it for mild-to-moderate cases), *Crinum latifolium* (*náng hoa trắng*), *Houttuynia cordata* (*diếp cá*), and *Morinda officinalis* (*ba kích*) are all routine *Thuốc Nam* prescriptions with documented physiological activity but no clean place in Bios's medication-annotation surface.
- The Southern school's *acute febrile illness* repertoire is shaped by the tropical-disease catchment (dengue, malaria, chikungunya, typhoid, leptospirosis) the executive summary §1 named. The *Thuốc Nam* practitioner's diagnostic framework for differentiating these fevers from the more generic "wind-cold / wind-heat" patterns the *Thuốc Bắc* face would use is *finer-grained for tropical fevers* — and Bios's undifferentiated `infection_onset` pattern misses both the Northern-school wei-qi-ying-xue staging *and* the Southern-school tropical-fever differentiation.

**Distinctive practices that need surfacing:**

- ***Giác hơi* (cupping)** — fire-cupping and dry-cupping, applied along the *Du* (Bladder-channel parallel) line and at named indication points. Leaves circular ecchymotic marks. Measurable HRV and skin-temperature effects in the worked area for 12–24 hours. The cross-cultural diagnostic confound (ED misinterpretation as bruising / trauma) named in executive summary §5 applies.
- ***Cạo gió* (coin-rubbing)** — a coin (often a copper coin or a porcelain spoon) is dipped in mentholated balm and stroked along the back, neck, chest, and limbs until linear erythematous-to-petechial striae appear. Used for "*trúng gió*" (wind-strike, broadly: acute cold-onset malaise). The cross-cultural diagnostic confound is *especially* acute for *cạo gió* because the linear marks on a child's back look uncannily like an abuse pattern to a clinician unfamiliar with the practice.
- ***Bó thuốc* (herbal poulticing for fractures and contusions)** — traditional bone-setting includes external herbal application as well as manual reduction.

**Recommendation, Vietnamese-specific:**

1. Apply the [TCM audit](TCM_POV.md) recommendations as-is for the *Thuốc Bắc* face. They are not Vietnamese-specific but they cover most of the diagnostic surface.
2. Extend the medication-annotation surface to first-class the top ~30 *Thuốc Nam* plants with their botanical Latin (Plant List / WFO codes are the stable global identifier), Vietnamese vernacular name, and known cardiovascular / endocrine / hepatic / respiratory effects. Same shape the [African audit §2.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) recommended for African medicinal plants.
3. Add an `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric for *giác hơi* and *cạo gió* with affected-area annotation. The cross-cultural diagnostic-confound case is a real reason to make this explicit — the timeline annotation is *clinically protective* for the owner in an ED context.
4. Add `VN` to [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with DAV regulatory framing, the National Hospital of Traditional Medicine's clinical references, and the Vietnamese Ministry of Health's hypertension / diabetes guidelines.
5. Add Vietnamese to the language overlay priority list. Vietnamese has a relatively stable Unicode-Latin script (*Quốc ngữ*) and is one of the lower-engineering-cost localisations available.

### 2.3 Thai — *Phaet Phaen Boran*

Thai traditional medicine is institutionally framed under the Department for Development of Thai Traditional and Alternative Medicine (DTAM), which since 2002 has registered practitioners across four main branches:

- ***Phaet Phaen Boran* (general internal medicine and herbal pharmacy)** — diagnostic frame organised around *thātu* (the four elements, from Pali *Dhātu*: *paṭhavī* / earth, *āpo* / water, *tejo* / fire, *vāyo* / wind), with seasonal and constitutional adjustments. The National List of Essential Herbal Medicines (LIPHM) has been incrementally expanded since 1999 to cover the actively-used pharmacopeia.
- ***Phaet Phaen Phadung Khan* (midwifery)** — including pre- and post-natal care, *yu fai* (postpartum-confinement-with-heat therapy), and traditional birth attendance.
- ***Nuat Phaen Thai* (Thai traditional massage)** — recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2019. A structured 90-to-120-minute therapeutic massage following named *sen* (energy lines, broadly meridian-equivalent) and acupressure points; the Wat Pho lineage in Bangkok is the canonical reference.
- ***Phaet Phaen Saiyasart* (spiritual / esoteric medicine)** — includes sacred-water therapies, *yantra* (sacred geometry) practices, mantra recitation, and amulet practice. Practised within the Buddhist temple structure with monastic involvement.

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

***Nuad Thai* as a high-value intervention-event surface.** A *Nuad Thai* session has predictable measurable physiological effects: HRV elevation typically 60–180 minutes post-session; skin-temperature elevation in the worked muscle groups; sleep-quality improvement that night (deeper N3 fraction, fewer awakenings); transient orthostatic-tolerance change. The peer-reviewed evidence base is substantial (Buttagat; Saetia; Sukonthasarn; collaborations with Chulalongkorn and Mahidol). Bios collects every signal needed to read these effects, and currently has no event anchor to read them against. An owner who receives a 90-minute *Nuad Thai* on Tuesday afternoon should be able to log "*Nuad Thai* session, 90 min, full body" and have the wearable trace for the next 24 hours visible against that anchor. This is the cleanest single intervention-event case across the panel.

***Luk pra kob* (Thai herbal-compress therapy).** A hot herbal compress (typically *Curcuma longa*, *Zingiber cassumunar* / *plai*, *Cymbopogon nardus*, kaffir lime leaves) applied to specific anatomical regions. Measurable skin-temperature and HRV effects similar to but distinct from *Nuad Thai*. Same intervention-event recommendation applies.

**The *yu fai* postpartum tradition.** Thai postpartum confinement involves a structured 7-to-30-day "lying by the fire" period with heat application, dietary restriction, herbal steam, abdominal binding, and movement restriction. The wearable signature is distinctive: elevated baseline skin temperature, modified sleep architecture, restricted activity counts, distinct fluid-balance autonomic patterns. The [PhysiologyState](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/physiology/PhysiologyState.kt) `POSTPARTUM` enum value is too coarse to distinguish a *yu fai*-following Thai postpartum from a generic postpartum baseline; the engine's pattern reads will fire false-positives across the *yu fai* period if the engine is not aware of the practice. A sub-state or annotation that flags "*yu fai* / structured postpartum confinement" would let the engine adjust thresholds appropriately. This generalises beyond Thailand (similar practices exist in Vietnam — *ở cữ*; in China — *zuò yuèzi*; in Malaysia — *berpantang*; in the Philippines — modified versions in some lineages; in Indonesia — *pingitan* and related practices) and is a real candidate for a `PhysiologyState` extension.

**The Pali-derived *thātu* (four elements) frame.** Thai *phaet phaen boran* diagnostic reasoning organises around earth / water / fire / wind balance, with each element associated with specific body systems, seasons, and dietary/lifestyle adjustments. This is structurally analogous to the *Tridosha* (Ayurveda), *Nyepa* (Sowa Rigpa / Mongolian), and four-humour (Greco-Arabic Unani) systems, and the same pull-side overlay the [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.2](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [Ayurveda audit §2.1](AYURVEDA_POV.md) recommended for those traditions applies. The Thai version of the overlay would re-project the same HRV / RHR / sleep / temperature / glucose signals onto *paṭhavī* / *āpo* / *tejo* / *vāyo* axes.

**Saiyasart and the orthogonality boundary.** Thai sacred-water, *yantra*, amulet, and mantra practices are not biomarker-visible and were not constructed to be. The same boundary the [African audit §3.2](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named for Ifá divination applies here: Bios is an instrument for biomarker measurement, not a sacred-practice tracker. The panel does not ask Bios to integrate *saiyasart*; it asks Bios to preserve the [AlertContentPolicy.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt) restraint against interpretive language that would foreclose the *saiyasart* frame as a legitimate complementary practice.

**Recommendation, Thai-specific:**

1. `TH` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with FDA Thailand regulatory framing and DTAM clinical references.
2. `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric for *Nuad Thai* and *luk pra kob* sessions.
3. Extended medication-annotation for the LIPHM herbal pharmacy (top ~20 species).
4. `PhysiologyState` extension (or annotation) for structured postpartum confinement; this generalises across the panel.
5. Thai language overlay. Thai script is well-supported by Android; the engineering cost is mostly translation.

### 2.4 Khmer — *Kru Khmer*

Khmer traditional medicine in 2026 is a tradition in active reconstruction. The *kru khmer* practitioners who held the *kbun srok srè* (village-and-forest pharmacy), the *thmop* (manuscript medicine) lineage, and the Buddhist-temple medical traditions were specifically targeted during 1975–79; an estimated majority of senior practitioners were killed, and the *satra* (palm-leaf medical manuscripts) held in temple libraries were largely destroyed. What survives is partly-reconstructed through the work of the National Centre of Traditional Medicine in Phnom Penh, the Cambodian Traditional Healers Association, ethnobotanical projects (Ashwell, Walston; the *Nomad* documentary project; the Cambodia Tree Seed Project), and the substantial body of oral and monastic memory.

The audit treats this with the epistemological humility the situation calls for. The panel's Khmer member is explicit: "We are reconstructing what was taken. Some of what is taught now is what we remember; some is what we have rebuilt from comparable traditions in Thailand, Vietnam, and Lao PDR; some is honestly new work done in conversation with the tradition's living elders. An app that pretends the *kru khmer* tradition is a stable, fully-documented system would be inappropriate. An app that recognises the situation honestly is welcome."

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

**The Buddhist temple as the locus of medical practice.** Much of what is being reconstructed comes through the monastic structure — *wat*-based clinics, monk-practitioners, and the integration of medical and spiritual practice in a single setting. Bios's restraint against pushing interpretive language ([AlertContentPolicy.kt:51-83](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt#L51-L83)) supports the *kru khmer* + monastic setting in the same way it supports the *babalawo* + Yoruba-tradition setting in the [African audit](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md): the instrument reports, the practitioner-in-context interprets.

**The oral-transmission and reconstruction problem.** The audit does not ask Bios to participate in reconstruction. It asks Bios to *not pretend completeness*. The alert-text surface in the (eventual) Khmer language overlay should not refer the owner to "your healthcare provider" with the implicit assumption that the provider is a biomedical clinician. The translation choice in Khmer should support the broader formulation the [African audit §3.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) recommended: "consult a practitioner you trust." This honours the situation in which the *kru khmer* the owner consults may be working a recently-reconstructed lineage in a temple setting, alongside (or in lieu of) a biomedical clinician.

**Tropical-disease catchment.** Same as the executive summary §1: dengue, malaria, typhoid, melioidosis (endemic across Cambodia, particularly in rice-paddy-exposed populations), chikungunya. Same recommendation: regional disease-pattern overlay gated to the region config.

**No first-class surface yet for *thnam khmer* (Khmer medicine).** The reconstructed Khmer pharmacopeia includes Tinospora crispa (*voar* / *bandol pinpeay*), *Andrographis paniculata*, *Aquilaria* species (agarwood, used in some preparations), *Curcuma* species, *Centella asiatica*, *Phyllanthus* species, plus distinctive forest plants under active ethnobotanical documentation. The medication-annotation surface needs the same extension this audit recommends across all panel pharmacopeias.

**Recommendation, Khmer-specific:**

1. `KH` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with the Cambodian Ministry of Health regulatory framing and acknowledgment of the National Centre of Traditional Medicine.
2. Khmer language overlay. The Khmer script has full Unicode support, but the absence of word-break spaces in written Khmer is a layout consideration for the alert-text surface.
3. The tropical-disease overlay (executive summary §1) gated to the region.
4. Conservative posture on any *kru khmer* content — the audit explicitly does not recommend pre-populating a "Khmer traditional medicine knowledge base" because the tradition's living elders are still actively determining what the consensus is. The medication-annotation surface should remain free-text-friendly for Khmer entries, with botanical-Latin coding where available, and without an opinionated Bios-curated reference layer.

### 2.5 Lao — Lao Traditional Medicine

Lao traditional medicine is the least-codified system on the panel — there is no Lao equivalent of the Thai DTAM, the Vietnamese national hospital structure, the Burmese DTM, or the Indonesian BPOM Traditional Medicine division at the same institutional scale. The Institute of Traditional Medicine in Vientiane (under the Ministry of Health) has been formalising practitioner registration and pharmacy regulation incrementally since the mid-2000s, with WHO collaboration; the work is ongoing.

The Lao tradition is *syncretic* — it draws on Khmer, Thai, Sino-Vietnamese, and indigenous upland-ethnic-minority sources (the Hmong, Khmu, and other upland-Lao communities maintain their own herbal and ritual traditions distinct from lowland-Lao Theravada-Buddhist-framed practice). The panel's Lao member describes their pharmacy as "shelf-by-shelf — *Andrographis* from the Vietnamese repertoire, *Curcuma* from the Thai, *Aquilaria* from the Khmer, *Lagerstroemia* from local Lao practice. The patient gets what works for them; the lineage of the herb is secondary."

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

**Bonesetting and manual-therapy traditions.** Lao bonesetting and manual therapy carry the same *INTERVENTION_EVENT* + post-reduction-monitoring recommendation as the Mongolian *bariach*, the Yoruba bonesetter, the Indonesian *sangkal putung*, and the Filipino *paltik* lineages.

**Syncretic pharmacy.** The Lao practitioner's pharmacy will draw from multiple regional pharmacopeias on the same shelf. The medication-annotation surface should support botanical-Latin coding as the stable identifier, with multi-language vernacular names (Lao, Thai, Khmer, Vietnamese loanwords as appropriate) as secondary fields. This is the same shape as the African audit's herbal-annotation surface but the panel's Lao member emphasises that the *multi-tradition-source* attribute matters — a *Curcuma* preparation made by a Lao practitioner from a Thai-tradition recipe and a Lao practitioner from a Khmer-tradition recipe are different clinical artefacts.

**Tropical-disease catchment.** Same as executive summary §1, with particular relevance for malaria (especially upland border regions), dengue, scrub typhus, and melioidosis (Lao PDR is in the melioidosis-endemic belt and the disease is under-recognised). The regional disease-pattern overlay applies.

**Recommendation, Lao-specific:**

1. `LA` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with the Lao Ministry of Health regulatory framing.
2. Lao language overlay. Lao script has Unicode support; the language is closely related to Thai and the translation work has substantial transferable structure.
3. The same multi-source-herbal annotation infrastructure recommended for Vietnamese, Khmer, and Burmese practice.

### 2.6 Burmese — Department of Traditional Medicine

Burmese (Myanmar) traditional medicine is institutionally framed under the Department of Traditional Medicine within the Ministry of Health and Sports, with a 1996 Traditional Medicine Council Law providing the regulatory structure and University of Traditional Medicine in Mandalay providing the formal training pathway (a five-year BS degree). The DTM maintains hospital infrastructure, a national-scale practitioner registry, and a pharmacopeia under regulatory oversight.

The classical structure of Burmese traditional medicine — as taught in the University of Traditional Medicine curriculum — is the four branches noted in the executive summary §9:

- ***Desana***: theoretical and doctrinal medicine, drawn from the *Vedasathay* (Pali-derived medical canon), structured around three *Dhātu* (akin to the Ayurvedic *Tridosha* and the Thai *thātu*: *paṭhavī* / earth, *āpo* / water, *tejo* / fire, *vāyo* / wind — sometimes presented as three, sometimes four, with internal-curriculum variation). Internal medicine, pharmacy, diagnostic reasoning all sit here.
- ***Bethitza***: purification therapies, closely analogous to Ayurvedic *Panchakarma* in structure but with distinct Burmese-specific protocols. Emesis, purgation, oil therapies, and sweating regimens.
- ***Netkhatta***: astrological medicine. Determines auspicious timing for procedures, directional considerations for therapy administration, and constitutional reading based on *Nakshatra*-equivalent birth-time calculations.
- ***Vijjadhara***: alchemical and occult medicine. Includes *aggi-rasa* (mercury-and-mineral preparations, equivalent in structure to Ayurvedic *Rasashastra* but with Burmese-specific mineral pharmacy) and esoteric practices.

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

***Desana* maps onto the existing audit recommendations.** The internal-medicine and herbal-pharmacy face of Burmese DTM is structurally similar to Ayurveda (*Dhātu*-balance reasoning, three-humour framework) and shares the same audit recommendations: a pull-side humoural-overlay surface, a constitution-set option, season-aware baseline adjustment. The [Ayurveda audit](AYURVEDA_POV.md) findings on §2.1 (dosha model), §2.4 (Dinacharya), §2.5 (Ritucharya), §2.6 (Prakriti) apply directly to *Desana*; the Burmese practitioner can read those recommendations with the *Dhātu* vocabulary in mind.

***Bethitza* maps onto the intervention-event surface.** Same as Ayurvedic *Panchakarma* — a structured purification course is a bracketed clinical intervention with distinct pre / during / post wearable signatures. The [Ayurveda audit §2.12](AYURVEDA_POV.md) `TreatmentCourse` entity recommendation applies. The Burmese-specific protocols differ in detail but not in shape.

***Netkhatta* is partially orthogonal to biomarker measurement.** The astrological treatment-timing framework is *not* biomarker-derivable. The panel's Burmese member is explicit that the audit should not attempt to integrate *Netkhatta* — its function is to determine when and in what direction to administer a therapy, and the determination itself is made by the practitioner using astrological calculation, not by reading the patient's HRV. What Bios *can* do is what it already does: provide a clean intervention-event timeline that records *when* therapies were administered, so the post-administration physiological trace is available regardless of how the timing was originally chosen.

***Vijjadhara* is fully orthogonal and should remain so.** Burmese alchemical practice, including the *aggi-rasa* mercury-and-mineral preparations, is a closed lineage with its own internal safety conventions and is not appropriate for Bios to model in any capacity beyond the medication-annotation surface (an owner taking a *Vijjadhara*-prescribed preparation should be able to record the fact, with the same *self-reported provenance* the rest of the annotation surface uses). The audit explicitly does *not* recommend a Bios-curated reference layer for *Vijjadhara* preparations; the safety-and-pharmacology context is the practitioner's domain.

**Tropical-disease catchment.** Same as the executive summary §1, with particular relevance for malaria (Myanmar has the highest *P. falciparum* burden in the Greater Mekong sub-region and is the front line of artemisinin-resistance monitoring), dengue, chikungunya, and Japanese encephalitis. The regional disease-pattern overlay applies.

**Burmese script complication.** The audit names this as an engineering note: Myanmar Unicode versus the legacy Zawgyi encoding is a real complication that affects any `values-my/` overlay. The Myanmar Unicode transition has been in progress since the late 2010s; substantial fractions of the device-side population still use Zawgyi. The Bios localisation work needs to support both, or to make an explicit (defensible) choice for Unicode-only with documentation.

**Recommendation, Burmese-specific:**

1. `MM` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with the Department of FDA and Department of Traditional Medicine regulatory framing.
2. Burmese language overlay (Unicode, with Zawgyi-fallback consideration).
3. Apply Ayurveda audit §2.1, §2.4, §2.5, §2.6 recommendations as the *Dhātu*-framework substrate.
4. `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric covers *Bethitza* procedures.
5. Medication-annotation surface accepts *Vijjadhara* preparations as free-text owner-reported entries, with no Bios-curated reference layer (the practitioner is the reference).
6. Tropical-disease overlay gated to the region.

### 2.7 Indonesian — Jamu (and related *batra* practices)

Indonesian traditional medicine is among the most institutionally regulated on the panel. The 2012 Health Law and subsequent BPOM regulations establish a three-tier classification for traditional medicines:

- ***Jamu***: empirically-validated traditional preparations, historically used, with standardised raw materials but without modern clinical-trial data. Permitted on-shelf in *jamu* shops and via *jamu gendong* (the women who carry the daily jamu round on their backs in the morning streets).
- ***Obat Herbal Terstandar* (OHT)**: standardised herbal medicines with preclinical safety and quality data.
- ***Fitofarmaka***: fully-clinical-trialled phytopharmaceuticals, the highest tier; a small list, but growing.

The traditional-medicine landscape extends beyond *jamu* per se to include the *batra* (*pengobatan tradisional*) practitioners — *dukun bayi* (traditional midwives), *pijat* (massage practitioners, ranging from generalist to specialist *pijat refleksi* / reflexology and *pijat saraf* / nerve-massage), *sangkal putung* (bone-setters, with the most internationally-studied being the Sukoharjo / Central Java lineage), *jamu* practitioners (*peracik*) who compound preparations, and the regional variations across Java (Javanese, Sundanese, Madurese), Bali (with substantial Hindu-Balinese traditional medical practice including *balian* practitioners), Sumatra (*tabib* in Aceh with Greco-Arabic influence; Batak traditions; Minangkabau traditions), Sulawesi, and the eastern islands.

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

**The *jamu gendong* daily-life integration.** The Indonesian-traditional-medicine landscape is *uniquely embedded in daily life* on a scale that has no parallel elsewhere on the panel. The *jamu gendong* round — typically *kunyit asam* (turmeric and tamarind), *beras kencur* (rice and aromatic ginger), *gula asem* (palm sugar and tamarind), *paitan* (bitter herbs), *temulawak* (*Curcuma xanthorrhiza*) — is consumed daily by a substantial fraction of the urban-and-peri-urban Javanese population. The panel's *jamu gendong* member: "The patient takes jamu the way the person in London takes their morning coffee. It is daily. It is not a medical event in the patient's own framing — until they tell the doctor and discover that the doctor didn't know."

The medication-annotation surface needs to support *daily, low-intensity, routine* herbal intake — not just episodic prescriptions. The owner should be able to log a *jamu* regimen as a standing daily entry with a start date, and the engine should *not* fire alerts on the autonomic effects of routine *kunyit asam* consumption (anti-inflammatory, mild blood-pressure-lowering at chronic dosing, hepatic effects).

**The *sangkal putung* bone-setting lineage.** Same recommendation as the Mongolian *bariach*, Yoruba bonesetter, Lao bone-setter, Filipino *paltik*: a post-reduction-monitoring template with `PAIN_SCORE` trajectory, distal-warmth proxy, and compartment-syndrome escalation. The Sukoharjo lineage in particular is internationally studied (van der Sluis; *Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery*; ethno-orthopaedic literature) and the practitioners would value Bios in the same instrument-not-replacement frame.

**The *dukun bayi* traditional-midwife integration.** Indonesia has an explicit collaboration model between *Bidan Praktik Mandiri* (formal midwives) and *dukun bayi*, with the *Kemitraan Bidan-Dukun* programme dating to the 2000s. The pregnancy-state surface (per executive summary §8) needs the `attendantType` field to capture this; in the Indonesian context, the value `TRADITIONAL_BIRTH_ATTENDANT` would frequently be paired with `MIDWIFE_CERTIFIED` in a collaborative-attendance pattern that doesn't map cleanly onto a single value.

**Regional variation within Indonesia.** The Javanese-court *Kraton* (royal palace) traditions in Yogyakarta and Surakarta hold specific *resep keraton* (palace recipes) that are themselves distinct from the broader *jamu* tradition. Balinese *balian* practice integrates Hindu-Balinese religious framework with herbal and manual practice in a way that overlaps with Ayurveda but is not Ayurveda. Sumatran *tabib* in Aceh carries Greco-Arabic / Unani influence via the Indian Ocean trade routes. The audit does not recommend that Bios attempt to capture this regional variation in software — it is too much, and most of it is not biomarker-relevant — but it does recommend that any Indonesian language overlay support both Bahasa Indonesia (national) and Javanese (`values-jv/`, ~80 M speakers) at minimum, with the recognition that Sundanese, Balinese, Acehnese, and other regional languages also matter for vernacular access.

**BPOM-coded pharmacy.** Unlike most pharmacopeias on the panel, the Indonesian one has *formal regulatory coding* via BPOM registration numbers. A medication-annotation surface that accepts BPOM registration codes for *jamu* / OHT / Fitofarmaka preparations would give the engine a stable identifier and let the alert-explanation builder cite the regulatory tier. This is closer to the Japanese NHI extract codes (Tsumura numbers, Kotaro codes) the [Kampo audit §2.4](KAMPO_POV.md) recommended than to the looser free-text annotation surface most of the panel's pharmacopeias would use.

**Recommendation, Indonesian-specific:**

1. `ID` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with BPOM regulatory framing, including the Traditional Medicine Division's tier classifications.
2. Bahasa Indonesia language overlay; Javanese as a secondary priority.
3. Medication-annotation surface support for BPOM registration codes alongside botanical Latin and Indonesian vernacular names.
4. Medication-annotation surface support for *daily, standing* herbal intake as a distinct entry type from episodic prescriptions, with engine awareness that the autonomic effects of routine *jamu* consumption should not trigger alerts.
5. Pregnancy-state `attendantType` field supports the collaborative-attendance pattern.
6. Tropical-disease overlay gated to the region.
7. Apply the Ayurveda audit recommendations (§2.1 dosha, §2.5 Ritucharya) as the substrate for Balinese *balian* and Sumatran *tabib* practitioners on a tradition-by-tradition pull-side basis if and when those overlays are built.

### 2.8 Filipino — Hilot

Hilot is the umbrella term for Filipino traditional manual therapy and folk medicine, practised by *manghihilot* (the practitioner; sometimes *mananambal* depending on regional vocabulary, *mananggad* in some Visayan contexts) and including *paltik* (bone-setting), *pulso* (pulse-reading diagnosis), *pasubo* (offering/ritual elements), and *banyos* / *patapal* (poulticing). The tradition is regulated indirectly via the Philippine Institute of Traditional and Alternative Health Care (PITAHC), established under the 1997 Traditional and Alternative Medicine Act (TAMA), which has accredited practitioner training programmes and curated the DOH "10 Medicinal Plants" formulary that the Department of Health endorses for primary-care use.

Hilot is structurally distinctive on the panel in three ways:

- **Family-network healing structure.** Hilot lineages are typically passed within families, often with daughter-from-mother or daughter-from-grandmother transmission, and the practitioner is frequently a known family elder rather than a clinic-based professional. The patient-practitioner relationship is embedded in extended-kin networks in a way that the urban-clinic models on the rest of the panel are not.
- **Catholic-syncretic framing.** Filipino Hilot integrates Catholic religious framing — prayer, saint invocation, the use of blessed water and oil — alongside the manual and herbal practice. This is a syncretic structure where the religious and medical frames operate together rather than separately. The orthogonality boundary the [African audit §3.3](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named for spirit/ancestral etiology applies here in a different form: Bios is not asked to integrate the Catholic religious framing, but the alert-text surface should not foreclose it.
- **Rural barangay context.** The dominant Hilot deployment context is rural — barangay-level practice, often outside formal-clinic infrastructure, frequently the first-line care for fractures, sprains, childbirth, infant care, and broader manual-therapy complaints. Bios's offline-first, low-spec-Android, no-cloud-required architecture is structurally appropriate.

**Distinctive contributions and gaps:**

***Pulso* pulse-reading.** The *manghihilot*'s *pulso* reading is a less-elaborated taxonomy than Chinese 28-qualities — typically four-to-six qualities, with terms drawn from Tagalog and from Spanish-Latin-derived medical loanwords. The same discarded-PPG-morphology problem the [TCM audit §2.2](TCM_POV.md), [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.3](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [Kampo audit §2.7](KAMPO_POV.md) raised applies identically. The audit refers to those findings rather than repeating the analysis. The Filipino-specific note: a smaller pulse-quality vocabulary is *easier* to map cleanly to PPG-waveform features than the Chinese 28-qualities, which means the recommendation may be more tractable in a Filipino-context pilot than elsewhere on the panel.

***Paltik* bone-setting.** Same recommendation as the Mongolian *bariach*, the Yoruba bonesetter, the Indonesian *sangkal putung*, the Lao bone-setter: post-reduction-monitoring template with `PAIN_SCORE` trajectory, distal-warmth proxy, and compartment-syndrome escalation. The Filipino *paltik* lineage has substantial international documentation through ortho-trauma collaborative work (Salvanera; collaborations with University of the Philippines Manila and Philippine General Hospital).

**The *komadrona* / *hilot ng panganganak* (traditional birth attendant).** As noted in executive summary §8 and across the panel, traditional birth attendance is still common in rural Philippines, and the pregnancy-state `attendantType` field would let the *komadrona*-attended pregnancy be visible to Bios.

**The DOH "10 Medicinal Plants" formulary.** This is a unique feature of the Filipino regulatory landscape: the Department of Health has formally endorsed ten plants for primary-care use (*Lagundi* / *Vitex negundo*, *Sambong* / *Blumea balsamifera*, *Akapulko* / *Cassia alata*, *Yerba Buena* / *Mentha cordifolia*, *Tsaang Gubat* / *Carmona retusa*, *Niyog-Niyogan* / *Quisqualis indica*, *Bayabas* / *Psidium guajava*, *Bawang* / *Allium sativum*, *Ulasimang Bato* / *Peperomia pellucida*, *Ampalaya* / *Momordica charantia*) with specific indication guidance. This is a *finite, government-endorsed* list — easier to first-class in the medication-annotation surface than the larger and less-formal pharmacopeias of most of the panel.

**Catholic-syncretic framing and the alert-text register.** The Hilot framing of illness frequently includes spiritual-and-relational elements (*usog* / evil-eye-equivalent on infants; *pasma* / a wind-cold-related syndrome; *nuno sa punso* / mound-spirit-related affliction). These are not biomarker-visible. The audit applies the [African audit §3.3](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) recommendation: epistemological humility in the text. The Filipino / Tagalog / Cebuano language overlays should not blanket-recommend "consult your healthcare provider" — they should support the broader formulation "consult a practitioner you trust," recognising that the trusted practitioner in a barangay context is frequently the *manghihilot* (or the parish-priest-and-*manghihilot* combined).

**Recommendation, Filipino-specific:**

1. `PH` region in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) with FDA Philippines + PITAHC regulatory framing.
2. Filipino (Tagalog) language overlay as the national-language priority; Cebuano (`values-ceb/`) and Hiligaynon (`values-hil/`) as priority secondary overlays for the Visayan and Mindanao regions where they are the household-vernacular language.
3. Medication-annotation surface first-classes the DOH "10 Medicinal Plants" list with botanical Latin codes and Filipino vernacular names.
4. `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric supports Hilot manual-therapy sessions.
5. Post-reduction-monitoring template for *paltik*-served owners.
6. Pregnancy-state `attendantType` field supports *komadrona* attendance.
7. Tropical-disease overlay gated to the region (Philippines has dengue endemicity, malaria in specific southern islands, leptospirosis particularly post-typhoon, scrub typhus in rural areas).

## 3. Manifesto / panel tension points

These are *not* gaps. They are places where the manifesto's commitments and the traditions on the panel produce different answers, and the audit names the differences rather than smoothing them.

### 3.1 "The owner is final" vs. family-and-community decision frames — same tension as the African audit

Manifesto Principle 7 centres the *individual* owner as decision-maker. This is correct for the contemporary biomedical, consumer-protection, and GDPR-compatible posture Bios has chosen, and it is correct for the manifesto's autonomy commitment. The same tension the [African audit §3.1](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) raised applies across most of this panel:

- Filipino Hilot practice is *family-network-situated* — the patient's *kapamilya* (family) is frequently part of the consultation, the practitioner is often a family elder, and decision-making is collective in a way that the individual-centric framing does not capture.
- Indonesian *jamu* and *dukun bayi* practice frequently involves extended-family input and intergenerational transmission of preparations and decisions.
- The Buddhist-framed traditions (Thai, Khmer, Lao, Burmese, Mongolian) carry a *sangha*-and-family framework in which the monastery, the extended family, and the patient together carry the situation. The patient is not the sole decision-maker by tradition.
- Vietnamese family structure around illness includes substantial extended-family involvement, particularly for elder care and for paediatric situations.

The audit applies the African audit's resolution: the data-sovereignty side of "the owner is final" is correct everywhere and the panel endorses it; the decision-making side is correct for biomedical decisions and for the post-Dobbs / authoritarian-state threat model; the consent-to-share-with-family surface should be extended to recognise that "with my family" is a legitimate owner-chosen sharing mode for owners whose traditions situate decision-making collectively.

### 3.2 Buddhist / animist framings and the "non-judgement" register

Several traditions on the panel operate within an explicit Buddhist or Buddhist-animist framework: Mongolian Vajrayana, Thai / Khmer / Lao / Burmese Theravada, and the Hindu-Buddhist syncretic framework of Balinese practice. The Buddhist register of medicine is *non-judgmental* in a distinctive way — the body and its conditions are read as *anicca* (impermanent), *dukkha* (unsatisfactory), and *anatta* (without fixed self), and the practitioner's posture is one of supportive presence rather than evaluative judgment.

This is *exceptionally well-aligned* with the [AlertContentPolicy.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt) CI-gated banlist on "you should / you need to / streak / level up." The panel notes that Bios is, in this sense, *more Buddhist-medicine-compatible* than most consumer health software, and that the alignment is structural rather than performed. The audit recommends only that the alignment be preserved deliberately as Bios scales — the temptation to add "encouragement," "gamification," or "behavioural-nudging" features as a growth lever should be refused, both because the manifesto refuses them and because the Buddhist-medicine catchment specifically values their absence.

### 3.3 Catholic-syncretic and Islamic framings — orthogonality named honestly

Filipino Hilot's Catholic-syncretic framing, and the Islamic framing that overlays substantial parts of the Indonesian (Aceh, much of the rest of Sumatra, parts of Java), Malay, and southern-Philippine (Mindanao) populations, are *orthogonal* to biomarker measurement in the same way the [African audit §3.2](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) called Ifá divination orthogonal. Religious framings of illness — *bala* (Tagalog: divine trial), *kismet* (Islamic frame: divine apportionment), the Catholic frame of suffering as redemptive — are not biomarker-visible and were not constructed to be. Bios is not asked to integrate any of this. The audit asks only that the [AlertContentPolicy.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt) restraint against interpretive language be preserved deliberately, so the alert text does not foreclose religious framings of the owner's condition.

### 3.4 The "free to all" commitment and the *dāna* / *zakāt* / Buddhist-generosity register

The Buddhist *dāna* ethic (formal in Mongolian, Thai, Khmer, Lao, Burmese practice), the Islamic *zakāt* / *sadaqah* obligation (relevant for Indonesian, Malaysian, southern-Philippine Muslim populations), and the Catholic *caritas* tradition (relevant for the Philippines) all frame medicine as service rather than market. The manifesto's Principle 3 — "no one should miss an early detection because they cannot afford a subscription" — restates this in modern terms. The alignment is not accidental.

The audit applies the [Sowa Rigpa audit §3.4](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [TCM audit §3.4 ("Free to all — Sun Simiao's 大医精诚")](TCM_POV.md) framing: this is the same vow expressed in software ethics, and a contribution-popup with feature gates or donor differentiation would violate the panel's religious-and-cultural framing as severely as it would violate the manifesto. The alignment should be preserved deliberately.

## 4. Latent strengths the codebase has not yet named

These are places where Bios is *already aligned* with the panel's deployment-context concerns, and where the audit recommends only that the alignment be named so it can be preserved.

### 4.1 Mobile-Android-first architecture matches Southeast Asian and Mongolian mobile-health context

Southeast Asia and Mongolia have world-leading mobile-money and mobile-health adoption — Indonesia's GoPay / OVO / DANA, Vietnam's MoMo / ZaloPay, the Philippines' GCash / PayMaya, Thailand's PromptPay, Mongolia's Khan Bank app and the wider banking-app ecosystem. Mobile-health adoption is similarly substantial: Indonesia's *Halodoc* and *Alodokter*, Vietnam's *eDoctor*, Thailand's *Mor Dee*, the Philippines' *KonsultaMD*, plus the WHO Western Pacific Region's mobile-health framework guidance.

The structural conditions parallel the African context: low-cost Android on a 2018–2022-class chipset is the dominant computing surface; data is metered (less so than in much of Africa, but still material); a substantial fraction of devices outside the major-urban-centres are degoogled or Huawei-AppGallery-only; Bluetooth pairing with cheap peripheral devices works better than Wi-Fi cloud sync.

Bios's architecture — Android 9+, no Google Play Services dependency, no cloud dependency, offline-first, SQLCipher local storage, optional BLE peripheral integration — is *exactly* the architecture this context calls for. The panel asks that this alignment be named in the project's positioning, as it is one of the few consumer health products whose technical architecture is *structurally* appropriate for Southeast Asian and Mongolian deployment without retrofitting.

### 4.2 The local-first storage as anti-biopiracy posture — extended from the African audit

The biopiracy history that shaped the [African audit's §4.2](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) data-sovereignty framing has direct Southeast Asian parallels. The *Andrographis paniculata* commercialisation history (multiple Western patents over the past two decades on isolates from a plant used continuously in Vietnamese, Thai, Indonesian, Filipino, Khmer, Lao, and Burmese pharmacopeias). The *Eurycoma longifolia* (*tongkat ali*, Malaysian / Indonesian) bioprospecting and patenting history. The Indonesian government's 2007 *Nagoya Protocol*-precursor stance on viral-sample sharing (the H5N1 dispute with WHO). The Indonesian *jamu* protection efforts under BPOM. The Thai *Plant Variety Protection Act* and the *Protection and Promotion of Traditional Thai Medicinal Intelligence Act* (1999). The Mongolian Ministry of Health's traditional-medicine intellectual-property work.

The panel notes the same alignment: Bios's local-first architecture, encrypted-export-on-owner-action sharing model, and differential-privacy-noise-on-community-contributions posture are structurally protective in exactly the way the panel's national regulatory frameworks have been calling for. The alignment should be named in project documentation.

### 4.3 The intervention-event surface this audit recommends serves multiple traditions

The `INTERVENTION_EVENT` metric recommended throughout this audit (Mongolian *chimedeg* and *bariach*; Vietnamese *giác hơi* and *cạo gió*; Thai *Nuad Thai* and *luk pra kob*; Khmer manual practices; Lao bone-setting; Indonesian *pijat* and *sangkal putung*; Filipino Hilot and *paltik*) is a *single architectural surface* that serves all eight traditions, with locally-relevant intervention-type vocabulary. It also serves the Sowa Rigpa *me btsa'* / *gtar ga* / *gser khab* / *bsku mnye* surface the [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.7](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) recommended, the Ayurvedic *Panchakarma* surface the [Ayurveda audit §2.12](AYURVEDA_POV.md) recommended, and the Burmese *Bethitza* surface this audit recommends. One architectural change, eight-plus traditions served. The leverage is significant.

### 4.4 The structured-postpartum-confinement state generalises across the panel

The `PhysiologyState` extension (or annotation) for structured postpartum confinement — Thai *yu fai*, Vietnamese *ở cữ*, Chinese *zuò yuèzi*, Malay / Indonesian *berpantang* / *pingitan*, Khmer *sor sai*, and similar practices across the panel — is another single architectural change that serves multiple traditions. The wearable signature of structured confinement (elevated baseline temperature, restricted activity, modified sleep, distinct fluid-balance autonomic patterns) is *measurable* across all of these, and the engine's pattern reads will fire false-positives across all of them if the engine is not aware. One extension, one shared signature, multiple traditions served.

## 5. Recommendations, tiered

**Tier A — owner safety and basic regional fit, ship before any further pattern expansion**

Recognises that "the practitioner" may be a *thầy thuốc Đông y*, a *phaet phaen boran*, a *kru khmer*, a *manghihilot*, a *jamu peracik*, a Burmese DTM practitioner, a *bariach* — not only a "doctor."

13. **Pull-side humoural overlays**, per [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.2](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [Ayurveda audit §2.1](AYURVEDA_POV.md), applied to *Dhātu* (Thai *phaet phaen boran*, Burmese DTM) and *khii* / *shar* / *badgan* (Mongolian *anagaakh*).
14. **Post-reduction monitoring template** for *bariach* / *sangkal putung* / *paltik* / Lao bone-setter / Vietnamese bone-setter served owners. Generalises the [African audit §4.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) bonesetter recommendation.
15. **PPG-waveform morphology preservation** per [TCM audit §2.2](TCM_POV.md), [Sowa Rigpa audit §2.3](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md), [Kampo audit §2.7](KAMPO_POV.md). Same architectural change; serves Filipino *pulso*, Vietnamese *Thuốc Bắc* pulse reading, and (less elaborately) Thai / Burmese / Mongolian pulse traditions.

**Do not adopt**

- A *Vijjadhara* / *saiyasart* / Hilot *pasubo* / Khmer ritual-practice tracker. The orthogonality boundary the [African audit §3.2 and §3.3](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named for divination and spirit etiology applies identically to esoteric, alchemical, and ritual practices across this panel.
- A Bios-curated *Vijjadhara* or *Rasashastra* mercury-and-mineral preparation reference layer. The safety-and-pharmacology context is the practitioner's domain; the medication-annotation surface stores what the owner reports, full stop.
- A pre-populated "Khmer traditional medicine knowledge base." The tradition's living elders are still actively determining reconstruction consensus; the audit refuses to overclaim coverage on their behalf.
- An automated *pulso* / *myakushin* / Vietnamese-pulse / Mongolian-pulse / Thai-pulse classifier. The PPG-waveform features should be *preserved and displayed*, not auto-classified — same recommendation as the [TCM audit §4](TCM_POV.md) and [Sowa Rigpa audit §4](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md).
- A push-side "you should consult a traditional practitioner" recommendation. The owner is final; the practitioner is the owner's choice; Bios reports observations and lets the owner decide who to consult about them.
- A "validate vs. invalidate" stance on any tradition on the panel. Bios is an instrument. Instruments do not adjudicate epistemologies. The instrument reports what it sees and stops.

## 6. Summary line for the project

> Bios is, at the level of architectural posture, **substantially compatible with the deployment-context realities of all eight traditions on this panel** — local-first storage, no-Google-Play-Services dependency, offline-first operation, mid-tier-Android baseline, no premium tier, the manifesto's restraint against unsolicited evaluation, and the encrypted-export-on-owner-action sharing model are *exactly* the technical commitments that the Mongolian steppe, the Mekong river valleys, the Java lowlands, and the Filipino barangay context have been calling for from consumer health software. The content layer is implicitly Northern-temperate, in the same way the [African audit](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) identified: no tropical-disease patterns, no region configs for any country represented on the panel, no language overlays for any of the panel's vernacular languages, no first-class surface for the panel's pharmacopeias, no `INTERVENTION_EVENT` anchor for the panel's substantial manual-therapy and intervention traditions, no postpartum-confinement physiology state for the practices that span Thai *yu fai*, Vietnamese *ở cữ*, Malay *berpantang*, and equivalents. None of the gaps require the manifesto to retreat. Several of the *non-gaps* — the orthogonality between biomarker observation and Burmese *Vijjadhara* alchemy, Thai *saiyasart*, Hilot *pasubo*, Khmer ritual purification, Indonesian *jamu*'s relational *gendong* round — are correct silences for an instrument of biomarker measurement and should be preserved deliberately. The audit's single most important recommendation is the *combined* one: tropical-disease regional overlay, region configs for the panel's countries, language localisation pipeline, a single `INTERVENTION_EVENT` surface that serves at least eleven traditions across this and prior audits, the structured-postpartum-confinement physiology state that serves at least five traditions, and the traditional-pharmacy annotation surface that supports botanical-Latin coding with multi-language vernacular fields — shipped together as a "Bios across mainland and maritime Southeast Asia, and the Mongolian steppe" capability that names the alignments that already exist and closes the content gaps that do not.
