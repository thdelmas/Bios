# Indigenous Americas Audit — Bios Read by Practitioners of the Hemisphere's Living Medical Traditions

**Scope:** Bios viewed not from a single tradition but from the *plural* landscape of Indigenous medical knowledge across the Americas — the hundreds of sovereign Indigenous nations whose medical practices remain in living continuity from the Arctic to Tierra del Fuego. Read by an explicitly non-singular panel: a Navajo *Hataałii* (Diné chanter / singer trained in the Beauty Way, Night Way, or Enemy Way ceremonial cycles), a Cherokee herbalist (Tsalagi *didanawisgi*), an Anishinaabe *Midewiwin* member (Grand Medicine Society of the Three Fires), a Lakota *wičháša wakȟáŋ* (holy man whose practice includes *inípi* sweat-lodge and *haŋbléčheya* vision-quest), an Inuit *Angakkuq* (Arctic — spirit-helper tradition), a Mexican-American *curandero/a* and the associated specialist trades (*sobador/a*, *huesero*, *partera*, *yerbero/a*), a Yucatec Maya *H'men* (ritual healer working in continuity with the Codex Badianus tradition), a Nahua *tepahtiani* (Mesoamerican generalist healer), an Andean *Kallawaya* (Bolivian itinerant herbalist tradition — UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage 2008), an Amazonian *vegetalista/ayahuasquero* (working within UDV, Santo Daime, or independent mestizo-vegetalista lineages), a Mapuche *Machi* (Chile/Argentina — *kultrun* drum, spirit-illness diagnostic frame), a Quechua *Hampiq*, and a Guaraní *Paje*.
**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Branch:** `feat/metric-info-sheets-on-read`
**Lens:** Indigenous Americas medicine, *plural*. The thirteen practitioner perspectives above do not collapse into a single voice and the panel does not pretend they do. Where they converge, this audit says so. Where they diverge, both readings are reported. Where Bios's biomarker frame is *orthogonal* to a practitioner's diagnostic frame, that is named as orthogonality — not a defect to be repaired.
**Auditor:** Claude (Opus 4.7)

This audit does **not** ask Bios to encode the *Diné Bahaneʼ* cosmology, the *Popol Vuh*, the *Q'ero* prophetic tradition, or any other Indigenous epistemology in code. It asks where, viewed through each of the practitioner frames on the panel, Bios's existing observations are usable, where they are silent, where Bios is *structurally aligned* with Indigenous data-sovereignty frameworks in ways the project's documentation has not yet named, and where the differences are differences of *kind* that should be acknowledged rather than smoothed over.

## A note on plurality, naming, and refusal of homogenisation

There is no such thing as "Indigenous American medicine" in the singular. There are hundreds of sovereign nations — Diné, Tsalagi (Cherokee), Anishinaabek, Lakota, Haudenosaunee, Mvskoke, Inuit, Inupiaq, Yupik, Mexica/Nahua, Yucatec Maya, K'iche', Mam, Mixe, Zapotec, Purépecha, Wayuu, Quechua, Aymara, Kallawaya, Shipibo-Konibo, Asháninka, Yanomami, Kayapó, Mapuche, Selk'nam, Guaraní, and many more — each with its own medical knowledge, transmission lineage, diagnostic vocabulary, pharmacopoeia, ceremonial cycle, and relationship (often a fraught one) to the settler state under whose laws their practitioners now operate. To group them under one banner is to repeat the colonial gesture that produced the term "Indian" in the first place. This audit groups by hemisphere and sub-region only because Bios's region-config layer is geographic; it does not group by method.

The audit uses each tradition's own terms for itself: *Hataałii* (Diné chanter), *didanawisgi* (Cherokee — herbalist), *Midewiwin* (Anishinaabe Grand Medicine Society), *wičháša wakȟáŋ* (Lakota holy man), *Angakkuq* (Inuit ritual specialist), *curandero/a* / *sobador/a* / *huesero* / *partera* / *yerbero/a* (Mexican-American spectrum), *H'men* (Yucatec Maya), *tepahtiani* (Nahua), *Kallawaya* (Andean), *vegetalista* / *ayahuasquero* (Amazonian mestizo and Indigenous-lineage), *Machi* (Mapuche), *Hampiq* (Quechua), *Paje* (Guaraní). It does not use "shaman" except when quoting an existing literature term, and it does not use "witch doctor," "medicine man" (except in tradition-internal idiom where the practitioner self-identifies that way in English), or "folk medicine" — those terms misname most of what is in this audit.

This audit also does not adjudicate. It does not validate or invalidate any tradition's diagnostic claims, etiological frames, or therapeutic methods. Bios is an instrument; instruments do not arbitrate epistemologies. Where this audit names a "gap," it means a gap in *what Bios's biomarker frame can usefully see and report*, not a deficiency in any tradition.

## Executive summary

Bios is, at the level of *architecture*, **the closest fit to Indigenous data-sovereignty frameworks of any consumer health product the panel has read.** The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics — Carroll et al. 2020), the OCAP® principles (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession — adopted by the First Nations Information Governance Centre in Canada), the United States Indian Health Service data-sharing posture, the Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board framework, the Native BioData Consortium's protocols, and the *Te Mana Raraunga* Māori data-sovereignty articulation (cross-pollinated with several Americas frameworks) all converge on a set of architectural commitments: data stays under the data-subjects' control, by default and as default; collective consent governs collective use; raw data does not leave the community without explicit, revocable authorisation; and the infrastructure must work without dependence on extractive cloud platforms. Bios's local-first SQLCipher storage, no-cloud-by-default posture, no-Google-Play-Services dependency, encrypted-export-on-explicit-owner-action sharing model, and refusal to monetise health data are *exactly* those architectural commitments — built for a different stated reason (personal autonomy, LETHE threat model) but landing on the same shape. This alignment is genuinely strong, genuinely underrecognised in the project documentation, and the panel asks first that it be *named*.

At the level of *content*, Bios's pattern library is implicitly mid-latitude temperate and implicitly attuned to a sedentary office-worker baseline. It has no model of altitude (a fatal gap for the Andean *Kallawaya*'s catchment, which overlaps the [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.1](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) altitude analysis almost line-for-line — Qosqo / Cusco at 3,400 m is in the same SpO2-baseline regime as Lhasa). It has no model of ceremonial physiology — the elevated heart rate, fasting, thermoregulatory stress, dehydration, and sustained ritual exertion of *inípi* (Lakota sweat lodge), the Sun Dance, *haŋbléčheya* (vision quest), *peyote* ceremony in the Native American Church, or *ayahuasca* ceremony in UDV/Santo Daime contexts are *exactly* the physiological signatures Bios's `infection_onset`, `cardiovascular_stress`, and `EmergencyVitalPatterns` would interpret as developing illness or acute emergency. There is no `H'men`-readable share format, no Amazonian Spanish or Quechua or Aymara or Yucatec Maya or Diné Bizaad localisation, no `MX` / `BO` / `PE` / `CL` / `AR` / `EC` / `CO` / `GT` region config in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) (the EU fallback silently substitutes ESC/ESH cut-offs for any of these locales). The screening catalog in [ScreeningCatalog.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt) presumes a US Indian Health Service / USPSTF cadence; there is no acknowledgment that for the half of the panel working in Latin America, USPSTF is not the relevant guideline. The [Applicability](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt#L10-L17) enum's `PRESENTS_AS_FEMALE` / `PRESENTS_AS_MALE` binary makes no room for two-spirit (Diné *nádleehi*, Zuni *lhamana*, Lakota *winkte*, Mojave *alyhâ* / *hwame*), third-gender, and fourth-gender identities that many nations recognise as distinct categories rather than as variants of the binary.

Beyond the implementation gaps, there are *fundamental philosophical orthogonalities* the panel agrees are not Bios's to fix. Diagnosis by *Hand-trembling* (Diné *ndilniihii*), *Star-gazing* (Diné *déétʼíʼii*), *Listening* (Diné *iisti*) — the three Diné diagnostic specialties — is not a less-precise version of biomarker measurement; it is a different epistemology entirely. The *Machi*'s reading of *kultrun* drumming sequences during *machitun* ceremony, the *H'men*'s interpretation of *sastun* (crystal-divining stone) configurations, the *Kallawaya*'s coca-leaf reading (*coca quintu*), the *ayahuasquero*'s *icaros* (medicine songs) sung to a specific patient on a specific night, and the *Midewiwin* fourth-degree healer's work with *Mide* scrolls — these are diagnostic and therapeutic practices in their own traditions. Bios reads HR and HRV; the *Hataałii* sings the Night Way over nine nights. These procedures answer different questions, and the panel is unanimous that Bios should not pretend otherwise.

Ordered by impact within the things Bios *could* address without overreaching, the gaps are:

1. **No ceremonial-physiology context, and the URGENT escalator will mis-fire during ceremony.** [EmergencyVitalPatterns.tachycardiaCritical](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/EmergencyVitalPatterns.kt#L122-L144) fires at sustained RHR ≥130; [spo2Critical](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/EmergencyVitalPatterns.kt#L58-L80) at SpO2 ≤85 %; [infection_onset](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt#L127) fires on the convergence of skin-temperature rise + RHR elevation + HRV depression. *Inípi* sweat lodge produces sustained HR elevation often into the 130–160 range, skin-temperature elevation of 2–4°C, dehydration with downstream HRV depression, and SpO2 dips with re-breathing in the closed lodge. The Sun Dance is days of fasting + sustained exertion + sleep deprivation. *Haŋbléčheya* vision-quest is multi-day fasting in solitude. Peyote ceremony in the Native American Church is an all-night ceremonial cycle with sustained alkaloid-modified autonomic state. Ayahuasca ceremony produces several hours of altered autonomic state with characteristic HR and HRV signatures. *Without ceremonial-context annotation, Bios will interpret all of these as escalating emergencies* — and worse, the false alerts will train participants to dismiss notifications, eroding the URGENT tier's signal value when a real emergency does occur. This is the single highest-priority gap for the North American panel, and it has no analogue in the [SOWA_RIGPA_POV](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) audit.

2. **No altitude modelling — same gap the Sowa Rigpa audit named, different catchment.** The Andean *Kallawaya* tradition operates in Bolivia at 2,500–4,500 m; the *Hampiq* across the Quechua-speaking highlands; *curanderismo* in the highland Mexican states (Puebla 2,135 m, Oaxaca, Chiapas) and into Guatemala (Quetzaltenango 2,330 m); the Mapuche *Machi* tradition in the Andean and pre-Andean Chile/Argentina at 200–2,000 m (lower stakes); even much of the US Southwest where Diné and Pueblo practice happens (Window Rock 2,070 m, Shiprock 1,550 m, Santa Fe 2,200 m, Taos 2,124 m). The fix is identical to the one [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.1](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) recommends and the audit cross-references rather than restates: owner-set `ELEVATION_BAND` annotation, per-band shift of [EmergencyVitalPatterns.spo2Critical](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/EmergencyVitalPatterns.kt#L58-L80) absolute cutoff. The mechanism the biomarker patterns already use (`absoluteBelow`) supports this directly.

3. **No region configs for the Americas south of the US.** [RegionConfigProvider.kt:199-209](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt#L199-L209) lists six regions: US, GB, EU, CA, AU, JP. There is no `MX`, `BO`, `PE`, `CL`, `AR`, `EC`, `CO`, `GT`, `BR`, `VE`, `PY`, `UY`, `CR`, `PA`, `HN`, `NI`, `SV`, `DO`, `CU`. A device locale set to `es_MX` returns `euConfig()` per the fallback at [RegionConfigProvider.kt:215-218](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt#L215-L218), which carries `regulatoryBody = "EMA"`, the CE-mark disclaimer, and ESC/ESH hypertension cutoffs. For Mexico the relevant regulator is COFEPRIS; for Brazil it is ANVISA; for Argentina ANMAT; for Chile ISP; for Peru DIGEMID; for Bolivia AGEMED; for Colombia INVIMA. None of those names appear anywhere in the codebase, and the EU disclaimer is meaningless to the user.

4. **No localisation into any Indigenous American language, and Spanish/Portuguese also absent.** [strings.xml](../../android/app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml) has no `values-es/`, `values-es-rMX/`, `values-pt-rBR/`, `values-qu/` (Quechua — ~8M speakers, official language in Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador), `values-ay/` (Aymara — ~2M, official in Bolivia and Peru), `values-gn/` (Guaraní — official in Paraguay alongside Spanish; ~6M speakers), `values-nah/` (Nahuatl — ~1.7M speakers), `values-yua/` (Yucatec Maya — ~800K), `values-nv/` (Navajo / Diné Bizaad — ~170K and the most-spoken Indigenous language in the US), `values-iu/` (Inuktitut — ~40K), `values-chr/` (Cherokee — ~2K active speakers with active revitalisation), or any of the dozens of others in active use. As with the [African audit §2.5](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md), the alert-text surface in [ConditionPatterns.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) is hardcoded in English (`explanation`, `suggestedAction`, `earlyDetection`, `prevention`, `healing`, `risks` fields — six paragraphs per pattern, 33+ patterns) and is not currently localisable in the existing architecture. The Spanish-language gap alone affects most of the Mexican-American *curandero/a* catchment and the entire Latin American panel.

5. **The screening catalog assumes USPSTF; no Indigenous Services Canada, no IHS, no Latin American cadence guidance.** [ScreeningCatalog.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt) is explicitly USPSTF-pinned (`val uspstf: List<ScreeningCatalogEntry>`). The doc-comment at [ScreeningCatalog.kt:50-53](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt#L50-L53) acknowledges this is v1 with regional alternatives as follow-up. For Indigenous owners in the US the *more* relevant cadence is the IHS Adult Preventive Care Bundle (which differs from USPSTF on diabetes screening interval, on hepatitis C screening prevalence-weighting, and on chronic kidney disease screening in adults with diabetes — all three are higher-prevalence concerns in IHS-served populations). For First Nations / Inuit / Métis owners in Canada the relevant frame is Indigenous Services Canada's Non-Insured Health Benefits programme, with provincial overlays. For Latin American Indigenous owners, the national MoH cadences plus the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) Indigenous-health-specific recommendations are the right reference. None of this exists in the catalog today.

6. **Binary `PRESENTS_AS_FEMALE` / `PRESENTS_AS_MALE` does not accommodate two-spirit, third-gender, fourth-gender identities.** [Applicability.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt#L10-L17) doc-comments itself as deliberately avoiding "identity politics on cadence math" by keying off organ-system presentation rather than gender identity, and the engineering rationale is defensible — a mammogram cadence is anchored to having breast tissue, not to a gender identity. But the language *as written* in the UI ([PreventiveCareScreen.kt:367-368](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/ui/screening/PreventiveCareScreen.kt#L367-L368): "Female-bodied" / "Male-bodied") forces a binary self-description on owners whose tradition recognises *more than two* gender categories. Diné *nádleehi*, Zuni *lhamana*, Lakota *winkte*, Crow *boté*, Mohave *alyhâ* and *hwame*, Kanaka Maoli *māhū* (Polynesian but the framing extends), Mexica/Nahua *cuiloni* (historically), Mapuche *machi weye* (historically and in some living practice) — these are not "non-binary" as a third option appended to the binary; they are categories the binary does not exhaust. The cleanest fix is to keep the organ-system-anatomy logic and re-label the UI: "Have breast tissue" / "Have a cervix" / "Have prostate / testes" as direct anatomy questions, not gendered body descriptions. This costs almost nothing and removes the gender-binary imposition.

7. **No surface for traditional pharmacopoeia annotation.** As with the African audit's §2.4, the [MedicationAnnotationRepo](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/data/MedicationAnnotationRepo.kt) added in Phase 7.4 is the natural home for a *yerbero/a*'s herbal annotations, a Cherokee *didanawisgi*'s formula entries, a *Kallawaya*'s coca / *muña* / *coca-mate* / *sangre de grado* annotations, and an Amazonian *vegetalista*'s plant-teacher entries (*ayahuasca* *Banisteriopsis caapi* + *chacruna* *Psychotria viridis*, *chiric sanango*, *bobinsana*, *uña de gato*, *sangre de drago*, *guayusa*, *tobacco mapacho*, the broader *vegetalista* dieta-tradition pharmacy). None of this has a structured entry surface today. A non-exhaustive list of plants with measurable autonomic / cardiac / metabolic / hepatic effects the panel would want annotated so Bios's pattern explanation builder reads in light of them: *Lophophora williamsii* (peyote — mescaline; sustained HR elevation, blood-pressure change, characteristic HRV pattern across 8–12 h), *Banisteriopsis caapi* + DMT-containing admixtures (ayahuasca — pronounced autonomic effect over 4–8 h, with characteristic emesis), *Nicotiana rustica* (mapacho — far higher nicotine than *N. tabacum*, ceremonial use in much of the Amazon), *Ilex guayusa* and *Ilex paraguariensis* (yerba mate — caffeine, theobromine, theophylline), *Erythroxylum coca* (coca-leaf chewing in the Andes — sustained but modest sympathomimetic; the Bolivian and Peruvian state regulatory frame distinguishes *acullico* from cocaine), *Psidium guajava* leaf (anti-diabetic effects), *Cinchona* spp. (quinine-containing — QT effects), *Catharanthus roseus* (also in the African audit; widely used in Mexican folk pharmacy for diabetes despite the vincristine-related contraindications), *Tagetes lucida* and *T. erecta* (Mesoamerican ceremonial), *Salvia divinorum* (Mazatec use, distinct from recreational), *Datura* spp. (powerful anticholinergic — the only one on this list that the panel asks be flagged as actively dangerous without practitioner supervision), *Brugmansia* spp. (similar to *Datura*), *Theobroma cacao* (ceremonial Mesoamerican preparations distinct from confectionery).

8. **`HealthEventType` enum, like the African audit found, presumes a biomedical encounter chain.** [Enums.kt:116-123](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/model/Enums.kt#L116-L123) has `DOCTOR_VISIT` as the only practitioner-encounter entry. An owner who consults the *Hataałii* for a Hand-trembling diagnostic, attends a sweat lodge led by a *wičháša wakȟáŋ*, consults a *curandera* about *susto* on a Friday, and then sees a biomedical primary-care doctor about hypertension the following Wednesday has four practitioner encounters and one event-type label that fits. The audit makes the same recommendation as the African one: rename to `PRACTITIONER_VISIT` and add a `practitionerType` annotation with values that include `MEDICAL_DOCTOR`, `INDIGENOUS_PRACTITIONER` (with sub-typing the owner provides as free-text rather than a closed enum — the panel is unanimous that Bios should not maintain a closed enum of Indigenous-practitioner role names), `MIDWIFE`, `BONESETTER`, `HERBALIST`, `OTHER`.

9. **Culturally specific syndromes in DSM-5-TR Cultural Concepts of Distress are invisible to Bios's mental-health pattern.** *Susto* (Mexican-American / Andean — fright-illness, often with somatic and affective symptoms; documented Pan-American distribution), *empacho* (gastrointestinal blockage, treated by *sobador/a* manual therapy and herbal teas), *mal de ojo* (evil-eye affliction, often diagnosed in infants), *bilis* or *muina* (anger-rooted gallbladder/liver complaint), *nervios* (chronic stress / dysphoria spectrum), *ataque de nervios* (acute Caribbean / Puerto Rican-origin presentation, DSM-5-TR listed), *pibloktoq* / Arctic hysteria (historically attributed to Inuit populations, contested in contemporary scholarship), *windigo / wendigo* psychosis (Algonquian — contested), *fallen-fontanelle / mollera caída* (Mexican-American infant syndrome treated by *partera* and *sobadora*), *latah* (Southeast Asian — not Americas, listed for contrast). The [mental_health_correlate pattern](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt#L424-L459) reads sleep + HRV + activity + circadian as a depression / anxiety proxy. As the [African audit §3.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) found, this *will* detect some of the somatic correlates of some of these syndromes — but the labels and suggested actions are biomedical, and the recommendation to "consult a healthcare provider" does not name *curandera* or *Hataałii* or *Midewiwin* practitioner as a culturally appropriate first consultation.

10. **The doctor-in-the-loop FHIR export is not legible to any practitioner on the panel.** Same finding as [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.10](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV §2.8](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) — the LOINC-coded FHIR R4 bundle is not a useful share format for a *Hataałii*, *curandero/a*, *H'men*, *Kallawaya*, *Machi*, or *ayahuasquero*. A printable A4 (or US-Letter — note that all of Latin America and Canada use A4; only the US uses Letter) summary in the device locale's language, with small-multiples time-series of the relevant signals over the last 14 days, the owner's own annotations on the timeline, and no diagnostic language, is the format the panel would actually use.

The rest of this audit walks the strengths, the gaps, the orthogonalities, and the manifesto-alignment points in detail.

## 1. What Bios already does well, viewed through Indigenous Americas frames

| Principle / structural posture | How Bios already embodies it | Why it lands with this panel |
|---|---|---|
| **Indigenous data sovereignty by architecture** | On-device SQLCipher; nothing leaves the device by default; encrypted export only on explicit owner action; no Play Services; community tier uses differential-privacy aggregation, not raw data | The CARE Principles (Carroll et al. 2020 — *Sci Data*), the OCAP® framework (FNIGC, Canada), the IHS data-sharing posture, Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board protocols, and the Native BioData Consortium's "data stays in community" stance all converge on architectural commitments that Bios already implements *for a different stated reason*. The alignment is real; the project documentation has not yet named it. This is the most important single observation in the audit |
| **No federated-learning shipping by default** | Federated-learning framework is roadmapped but the current state is local-only; community tier requires explicit per-owner opt-in (see [PrivacyTier](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/model/Enums.kt#L109-L112)) | Tribal IRBs and Indigenous data-governance bodies have well-founded historical reasons to be cautious about federated approaches that aggregate across communities without explicit collective consent. Bios's not-by-default posture leaves room for tribal-consent frameworks to be negotiated *before* any federated layer ships, which is the correct sequence |
| **Owner is final** | Manifesto Principle 7; [AlertContentPolicy](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt) CI-gated banlist of "you should / you need to / streak / level up"; pull-side surfaces dominate; no engagement metrics | For the individual-autonomy half of the panel this is a strong match. For traditions in which decision-making is relational (family, clan, ceremony, ancestors), see §3.1 — same tension the [African audit §3.1](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named, same resolution recommended |
| **Personal baseline, not population norm** | 14-day rolling per-metric baseline; z-score deviation; biomarker patterns gate on absolute cutoffs only where the literature threshold is unambiguous | Aligns with diagnostic frames across the panel in which the patient is the unit of comparison, not a normative table. A *Kallawaya* reading the coca quintu for *this* person, on *this* day, in *this* season, with *this* family situation, would recognise the individuated comparison as appropriate |
| **Convergence required before signal** | `minActiveSignals = 3` for [infection_onset](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt#L127); `required = true` gating on biomarker patterns; multi-signal cross-correlation | Mirrors the multi-sign convergence the Diné Hand-trembler waits for, the *H'men*'s reading across multiple *sastun* sessions, the *curandera*'s use of pulse + appearance + emotional state + family situation in a single consultation. The reluctance to alert on a single signal is the right reluctance |
| **Silence is a feature** | Push/pull split in [AlertContentPolicy.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt#L13-L25); no daily digest; no streaks; no nudges | Quiet observation is the practice many traditions on the panel train into — the *Angakkuq* listens before speaking; the *Hataałii* observes the patient and their relations before naming the chant; the *Midewiwin* fourth-degree healer is taught silence as a clinical posture. Bios's restraint matches |
| **Isolated reproductive database** | Separate SQLCipher key for `WOMENS_HEALTH` metrics; independent wipe; FHIR exporter skips reproductive by default; post-Dobbs threat model | Reproductive surveillance has its own colonial histories in the Americas — forced sterilisation programmes documented in IHS hospitals into the 1970s, the *Madres de Plaza de Mayo* contexts in Argentina, the contemporary criminalisation of pregnancy outcomes in much of the US South and parts of Latin America. The isolation generalises to any context where reproductive data is hostile to the patient's interests |
| **No telemetry, no analytics SDKs** | No Google Play Services, no Firebase, no Sentry-with-PII; the binary works in a fully degoogled environment | Indigenous communities in North America have well-documented experience with data being collected for one stated purpose and used for another (e.g., the Havasupai Tribe v. Arizona Board of Regents settlement over genomic samples collected for diabetes research and used for unrelated studies). Bios's no-third-party-SDK posture means the question "where else is this data going" has the architecturally correct answer: nowhere |
| **Free, no subscription gating** | Manifesto Principle 3 — "full health intelligence for everyone"; no Play Billing; same product for paid and community tiers | Aligns with IHS-eligibility logic (US), with provincial Indigenous health coverage (Canada), and with the *Sistema Único de Salud* / *Sistema Nacional Integrado de Salud* universal-coverage frames across Latin America. A subscription tier would silently exclude exactly the populations the IHS and equivalents were established to serve |
| **Works on low-spec, intermittently-connected Android** | API 28+; no cloud dependency; works offline; phone-sensor adapters cover the no-wearable case | The reality of mobile access on much of the rural Diné Nation (where the *Hataałii* practises), in the Bolivian altiplano (where the *Kallawaya* travels), in the Amazon basin (where the *vegetalista* works), in the Arctic (where the *Angakkuq* practises) is intermittent connectivity, older hardware, and unreliable power. Bios's architecture handles this without retrofitting |

The panel notes — and asks that the project notes — that several of these are *architectural commitments that Indigenous data-sovereignty frameworks have been arguing for over the last twenty years*. Bios did not build a "for-Indigenous" feature set; it built a privacy-first, owner-sovereign, low-spec-friendly architecture, and that architecture happens to be the architecture CARE / OCAP / Native BioData Consortium have been articulating. Naming this alignment explicitly in [PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md](../PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md) — adding a section on CARE / OCAP compatibility alongside the existing GDPR / HIPAA / Dobbs framing — would close a documentation gap without requiring a single line of code change.

## 2. Gaps, ordered by impact

### 2.1 Ceremonial physiology — the single highest-priority gap

This is the gap with no analogue in the [SOWA_RIGPA_POV](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) or [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) audits. Ceremonial practice across the Indigenous Americas produces physiological states that look, to Bios's current code, exactly like developing illness or acute medical emergency.

| Ceremony | Physiological signature | Bios's current reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Inípi (Lakota sweat lodge)** | Sustained HR 130–160+ for 30–90 min per round, 2–4 rounds; skin temp +2–4°C; sweat-loss dehydration; HRV depression; SpO2 dips with re-breathing in closed lodge | [tachycardiaCritical](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/EmergencyVitalPatterns.kt#L122-L144) fires URGENT; [infection_onset](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt#L127) fires on the convergence; [cardiovascular_stress](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) fires on the RHR↑+HRV↓ |
| **Sun Dance (Plains)** | 2–4 days of fasting + sustained exertion + sleep deprivation + thermoregulatory stress; some forms include flesh-offering | Bios would read a multi-day `recovery_deficit`, `metabolic_drift`, `cardiovascular_stress`, and potentially `infection_onset` — none of which is what is happening |
| **Haŋbléčheya (Lakota vision quest)** | 1–4 days of solitary fasting; minimal water intake; circadian disruption; sustained low-activity baseline with metabolic shift | `metabolic_drift`, `recovery_deficit`, possibly the depression branch of `mental_health_correlate` (sleep loss + activity drop + HRV depression) |
| **Peyote ceremony (Native American Church)** | All-night ceremony; sustained alkaloid effect 8–14 h with characteristic HR and HRV pattern from mescaline; mild blood-pressure elevation; nausea-related autonomic excursions | The HR and HRV signature does not cleanly match any condition pattern, but the all-night `circadian_phase_shift` + sleep loss + sustained HR elevation will register on the `mental_health_correlate` cluster |
| **Ayahuasca ceremony (UDV, Santo Daime, Indigenous-lineage)** | 4–8 h ceremony; characteristic autonomic shift; HR variation; emesis is part of the protocol (the "purga" is purposeful); sleep architecture shift the night of and after | Bios's current frame has no good reading; the emesis-related autonomic shift could approximate `cardiovascular_stress`; the night-of sleep architecture is wholly atypical |
| **Iboga (less common in Americas but used in some integrative-medicine contexts)** | Sustained QT effects, hours-long altered autonomic state | Would not match a single existing pattern but the cardiovascular signature is the safety-relevant one |
| **Andean despacho / pago a la Pachamama ceremonies** | Coca-leaf chewing producing sustained mild sympathomimetic effect; mountain-altitude cold-stress | Within already-named altitude gap |

**The safety design question is real:** how should Bios behave during ceremonial physiology? The panel's recommendation, on which it is unanimous:

1. **A `CEREMONIAL_CONTEXT` annotation** in the existing medication-annotation-adjacent surface — owner-set, time-bounded ("starts now, ends in N hours"), with named context types the owner picks (sweat lodge, sun dance, vision quest, peyote ceremony, ayahuasca ceremony, fasting ceremony, prayer ceremony, healing ceremony, other-described). Bios *never infers* ceremony; the owner sets the window explicitly, the same posture pregnancy and athletic-state already use.
2. **A `ceremonialActive` gate on the URGENT patterns and on `infection_onset` / `cardiovascular_stress`** — when the context is active, those patterns *do not push notifications*. The readings are still recorded, still visible on pull-side surfaces; only the push side is silenced. After the ceremonial window closes, a single "ceremonial window ended — readings during ceremony are preserved but were not interpreted" annotation appears in the timeline.
3. **A pull-side post-ceremony summary screen** — if the owner wants it, a quiet view that says: "during the *inípi* you logged, HR peaked at 168, SpO2 floor was 89%, skin temp rose by 3.4°C, returned to baseline within 90 minutes." No interpretation, no judgment, just the record. The *wičháša wakȟáŋ* or the *uncle* leading the ceremony can be shown this if the owner chooses.
4. **A safety carve-out**: if a vital sign crosses an *unambiguous* survival threshold (SpO2 <70 % sustained 10 min, HR >200 sustained 10 min, indications of acute cardiac event) the URGENT push still fires even in ceremonial context, with text that acknowledges the context: "These readings cross a survival threshold. If the ceremony leader is present, alert them; if alone or unsure, contact emergency services." This is the manifesto-aligned ceiling — Bios stays an instrument, but does not stay silent on a survival-threshold reading.

This is a Tier-A change. It is what makes Bios safe to wear during the ceremonies a substantial fraction of the panel's owners will be participating in. Without it, the URGENT tier the [MEDICAL_PROFESSIONAL_POV](MEDICAL_PROFESSIONAL_POV.md) audit asked to be wired up will train ceremonial participants to dismiss notifications — eroding the URGENT signal value precisely in the population most at risk of dismissing future real alerts.

### 2.2 Altitude — cross-reference to Sowa Rigpa audit

The Andean *Kallawaya* operates at 2,500–4,500 m across the Bolivian altiplano (La Paz at 3,640 m is in the same altitude band as Lhasa). The *Hampiq* across the Quechua-speaking highlands. Highland *curanderismo* in central Mexico (Puebla 2,135 m, Mexico City basin 2,250 m, Oaxaca, San Cristóbal de las Casas 2,200 m) and Guatemala (Quetzaltenango 2,330 m, Chichicastenango 2,030 m). Diné and Pueblo practice in the US Southwest (Window Rock 2,070 m, Shiprock 1,550 m, Acoma 2,150 m, Taos 2,124 m).

The fix is identical to [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.1](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and the audit does not restate it. The audit cross-references and amplifies: the altitude-modulated `EnvironmentalContext.elevationBand` annotation the Sowa Rigpa audit recommends serves the Andean Kallawaya catchment, the highland *curanderismo* catchment, and the US Southwest pueblo/Diné catchment without modification. A single piece of infrastructure closes three regional gaps simultaneously. Priority and engineering shape are identical.

Secondary altitude-adjacent concern unique to the Andean catchment: coca-leaf chewing (*acullico*) is a daily practice for a substantial fraction of the *Kallawaya* and *Hampiq*'s patient population, and it produces a sustained mild sympathomimetic effect that elevates RHR by 5–10 bpm and modestly suppresses HRV. The audit recommends this be handled via the herbal-medication annotation surface (§2.7), not as a special case.

### 2.3 No region configs for the Americas south of the US

[RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt) covers US, GB, EU, CA, AU, JP. There is no `MX`, no `BR`, no Spanish-speaking South America at all. A device locale set to `es_MX`, `es_BO`, `es_PE`, `es_CL`, `es_AR`, `es_EC`, `es_CO`, `es_GT`, `pt_BR`, `qu_PE`, `ay_BO`, or `gn_PY` falls through to `euConfig()`, which carries:

- `regulatoryBody = "EMA"` — meaningless in any Americas jurisdiction outside the EU. For Mexico the relevant body is **COFEPRIS**, for Brazil **ANVISA**, for Argentina **ANMAT**, for Chile **ISP**, for Peru **DIGEMID**, for Bolivia **AGEMED**, for Colombia **INVIMA**, for Ecuador **ARCSA**, for Guatemala **MSPAS**, for Paraguay **DINAVISA**, for Uruguay **MSP**, for Venezuela the regulator's status is contested. In Canada the Indigenous-health-specific layer adds **Indigenous Services Canada** alongside Health Canada.
- `alertDisclaimer` references CE-marking — not applicable.
- `hypertensionSystolic = 140` aligned to ESC/ESH. Latin American guidelines (LASH — Latin American Society of Hypertension, SAHA in Argentina, SBC in Brazil) variably align with ACC/AHA 130/80 or ESC 140/90; the Mexican GPC-IMSS guideline uses 140/90; the IHS guideline in the US uses ACC/AHA 130/80. The local reference is mis-sourced.
- `glucoseInMmol = true` is mostly correct across Latin America (most countries use mmol/L) but Mexico reports in mg/dL and the IHS in the US uses mg/dL.

**Recommendation:** at minimum, add `MX`, `BR`, `AR`, `CL`, `PE`, `BO`, `CO`, `EC`, `GT`, `PY`, and a `LATAM_DEFAULT` fallback for unmapped Latin American country codes. The IHS-specific layer for Indigenous owners in the US is a Tier-B item — it would carry the IHS preventive bundle and the IHS-specific disclaimer language. The Indigenous Services Canada layer for Indigenous owners in Canada is the same shape.

### 2.4 Localisation absent for Spanish, Portuguese, and every Indigenous American language

As with the [African audit §2.5](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md), the alert text in [ConditionPatterns.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) is hardcoded English and is not currently localisable. The localisation pipeline change (refactor text fields out of the data class and into `strings.xml`) is the same one-engineer-week of work the African audit recommended; the priorities for the Americas catchment:

1. **Spanish** (es-MX, es-AR, es-BO, es-CL, es-PE, es-CO, es-EC, es-GT, es-PY, es-UY, es-VE, es-DO, es-CU, es-HN, es-NI, es-SV, es-CR, es-PA — and Mexican Spanish is the largest single Latin American Spanish variant, but Andean Spanish, Rioplatense, and Caribbean Spanish all have local phrasings the panel would want). Spanish covers the vast majority of the *curandero/a* / *yerbero/a* / *sobador/a* / *huesero* / *partera* catchment, much of the highland Indigenous catchment as second language, and most of the Latin American Indigenous-state-citizen frame.
2. **Portuguese** (pt-BR) — for the Amazonian *vegetalista*, Santo Daime, UDV, and the Brazilian Indigenous nations whose state-citizen layer is Lusophone.
3. **Quechua** (qu — ~8M speakers; official in Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador; the linguistic family of the *Hampiq*) and **Aymara** (ay — ~2M; official in Bolivia and Peru; intimately tied to *Kallawaya* practice).
4. **Guaraní** (gn — official in Paraguay alongside Spanish; ~6M speakers across Paraguay, Bolivia, Argentina, Brazil; the linguistic family of the *Paje*).
5. **Diné Bizaad / Navajo** (nv — ~170K, the most-spoken Indigenous language north of Mexico; the linguistic frame of the *Hataałii*'s ceremonial transmission).
6. **Yucatec Maya** (yua — ~800K; the linguistic frame of the *H'men*) and **K'iche'** (quc — ~2M, the largest Mayan language by speakers, in Guatemala).
7. **Nahuatl** (nah — ~1.7M; the linguistic descent of the *tepahtiani* tradition and of much of the Codex Badianus pharmacopoeia).
8. **Inuktitut** (iu — ~40K; official territorial language in Nunavut; the linguistic frame of the Inuit *Angakkuq*) and the related **Inuinnaqtun**, **Inupiaq**, **Yupik**.
9. **Cherokee** (chr — ~2K active speakers but with active revitalisation programmes — Cherokee Nation Immersion School in Tahlequah, Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians programmes — and a unique syllabary that Android supports). Equivalent revitalisation contexts: **Anishinaabemowin / Ojibwe** (~50K), **Lakota** (~2K with revitalisation), **Cree** (~96K — the largest Indigenous language in Canada).
10. **Mapudungun** (arn — Mapuche language, ~250K speakers across Chile and Argentina; the linguistic frame of the *Machi*).

The audit does not ask Bios to ship all of these at once. It asks that the *architecture* support them (refactor text out of the data class) and that the *priority order* for which overlays ship first reflect speaker counts and tradition-linguistic alignment, not just market size. The panel notes: Spanish overlay alone has the largest single immediate impact across the Americas Indigenous-medicine catchment.

### 2.5 Screening cadence — USPSTF-only, no IHS, no Latin American MoH

[ScreeningCatalog.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt) is explicitly `val uspstf: List<ScreeningCatalogEntry>`. The doc-comment acknowledges this is v1 and that regional alternatives (NHS, Tokutei Kenshin, KCDC, BowelScreen AU) are follow-ups. For the Americas Indigenous-medicine catchment the relevant cadence overlays are:

- **IHS (Indian Health Service)** Adult Preventive Care Bundle — overlaps USPSTF substantially but diverges on diabetes-screening interval (annual after age 35 in IHS, three-yearly in USPSTF), on chronic kidney disease screening (annual in adults with diabetes per IHS, less aggressive in USPSTF), on hepatitis-C screening (universal one-time per USPSTF, with prevalence-weighted re-screening in some IHS regions), on suicide-risk screening (more aggressive in IHS given documented elevated burden), and on FASD-aware paediatric care (not applicable to adult catalog but relevant in adjacent surfaces).
- **Indigenous Services Canada / FNIHB** alongside provincial screening programmes.
- **Pan American Health Organization (PAHO)** Indigenous-health-specific recommendations — particularly relevant for cervical cancer (HPV-vaccine + screening cadence in Indigenous populations is a PAHO priority), Type 2 diabetes (substantially higher prevalence in many Indigenous American populations), trachoma in remote Amazonian communities, parasitic disease screening.
- **Mexican IMSS GPC** preventive cadence — Mexican-American owners on the US side may want their *curandera*-aligned consultation to reference the Mexican family-medicine cadence rather than USPSTF.
- **Brazilian SUS** preventive bundles.
- **Argentine Sociedad Argentina de Medicina** equivalent.

The mechanism for region-specific catalogs already has its hook in the existing code structure (the doc-comment at [ScreeningCatalog.kt:23-25](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt#L23-L25)). The Tier-B addition is to ship at least the IHS and the Mexican-IMSS catalogs as alternates the owner can select.

### 2.6 Two-spirit / third-gender / fourth-gender accommodation

[Applicability.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/screening/ScreeningCatalog.kt#L10-L17) is engineered correctly — the cadence math anchors to organ-system presentation, not to gender identity. The doc-comment is explicit: "not a sex/gender model for the owner. Trans / non-binary owners pick whichever recommendation set matches their organ-system presentation; the catalog doesn't impose identity politics on cadence math." That engineering choice is defensible.

The *UI labels* are not. [PreventiveCareScreen.kt:367-368](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/ui/screening/PreventiveCareScreen.kt#L367-L368) shows "Female-bodied" / "Male-bodied" — a forced binary in the description language. Many traditions on the panel — Diné *nádleehi*, Zuni *lhamana*, Lakota *winkte*, Crow *boté*, Mohave *alyhâ* / *hwame*, Cheyenne *heemaneh*, Mexica/Nahua historical *cuiloni*, Mapuche historical *machi weye* — recognise three or four gender categories that the female/male binary does not exhaust. "Two-spirit" is the contemporary pan-Indigenous English umbrella term adopted at the 1990 Winnipeg gathering of Indigenous gay, lesbian, bisexual, trans, and gender-diverse people; it is *not* a single gender identity but an umbrella, and many nations have their own term they prefer.

**Recommendation, which costs essentially nothing:** keep the organ-system-anatomy logic exactly as it is, and re-label the UI to ask the *direct anatomy questions* the cadence math actually needs:

- "Do you have breast tissue?" (gates mammogram cadence)
- "Do you have a cervix?" (gates HPV/Pap cadence)
- "Do you have a prostate?" (gates PSA discussion if/when added)
- "Were you ever pregnant?" (for retrospective cadence-relevant questions)
- "Have you ever smoked?" (gates AAA one-time screening together with age)

This removes the gender-binary imposition entirely. It is more accurate (the cadence math really does only care about anatomy), it makes room for two-spirit and other Indigenous gender categories without forcing anyone into an unfamiliar identity vocabulary, and it serves trans and non-binary owners better at the same time. The panel asks that this change be made.

### 2.9 Cultural concepts of distress — DSM-5-TR-listed and others

[The mental_health_correlate pattern](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt#L424-L459) reads sleep + HRV + activity + circadian + typing cadence as a depression/anxiety proxy. This is reasonable in a biomedical-psychiatry frame. The Americas panel's traditions diagnose differently. *Susto* (Mexican-American, Andean — fright-illness with somatic and affective symptoms), *empacho* (gastrointestinal blockage), *mal de ojo* (evil-eye), *bilis* / *muina* (anger-rooted hepatic complaint), *nervios* (chronic stress / dysphoria), *ataque de nervios* (Caribbean acute presentation — DSM-5-TR listed), *fallen fontanelle / mollera caída* (infant syndrome), the historically reported *pibloktoq* (Arctic, contested in contemporary scholarship), the historically reported *windigo* (Algonquian, contested), and several others appear in the DSM-5-TR Cultural Concepts of Distress section *precisely because* biomedical psychiatry has had to find a way to *not deny* the diagnostic validity of these frames within their communities of use.

Bios's `mental_health_correlate` will detect *some* of the somatic correlates of *some* of these — sleep disturbance, HRV depression, activity reduction — but the categorical label it produces ("mental health correlate") is biomedical, and the suggested-action text refers the owner to a "healthcare provider" rather than to the practitioner type their tradition would consult.

**Recommendation:** as with the [African audit §3.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md), this is not a request to add a "spirit-illness detector" — that would be inappropriate. It is a request that the Spanish, Quechua, Aymara, Nahuatl, Yucatec Maya, Diné Bizaad, Cherokee, Lakota, etc. overlays *not* blanket-recommend "consult your healthcare provider" but recommend, in the locally appropriate register, "consult a practitioner you trust." This is a translation choice, not a code change.

### 2.10 Print-ready practitioner share format

Same finding as [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.10](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) and [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV §2.8](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md). The FHIR R4 LOINC bundle is not a useful share format for the panel. A printable single-page summary (A4 for Canada and Latin America, US-Letter for IHS contexts) in the device locale, showing 14 days of small-multiples time-series of the relevant signals, the owner's own annotations on the timeline, and the active medication / ceremonial / pregnancy / altitude annotations as context bands, is the format the panel would actually use. ## 3. Manifesto / Indigenous-medicine alignment and tension points

These are *not* gaps. They are places where the manifesto's commitments and the panel's traditions produce the same answer (alignment) or different answers (tension), and the audit names which is which.

### 3.1 "The owner is final" vs. relational and ceremonial decision frames

Same tension the [African audit §3.1](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) named, same recommended resolution. Manifesto Principle 7 centres the individual owner as decision-maker. This is correct for the data-sovereignty side everywhere (the panel is unanimous that data-control must be individual-controlled because the ceremonial-context owner who needs Bios *not* to push notifications must be able to set that boundary unilaterally) and correct for the decision-making side in biomedical contexts where the owner may be acting against family pressure (a teenager hiding contraception use; a pregnant person concealing pregnancy in a coercive context; an LGBTQ+ owner in a hostile family environment).

For traditions in which decision-making is relational — Diné *k'é* (kinship as decision-frame), Lakota *tiyóspaye* (extended-family decision-making), *Midewiwin* clan and lodge decision-making, *Andean ayllu* community decision-making, many Amazonian Indigenous family-and-community frames — the recommendation is: keep the data-sovereignty side exactly as it is, and extend the sharing surfaces so "share with my family / clan / community of choice" is a first-class owner-initiated sharing mode alongside "share with my doctor." The owner-controlled, revocable consent surface should support that pattern, named in the language of relational decision-making rather than only in the language of biomedical referral.

### 3.2 Divinatory and ceremonial diagnosis is orthogonal to biomarker reading — correct silence

Hand-trembling (*ndilniihii*), Star-gazing (*déétʼíʼii*), and Listening (*iisti*) — the three Diné diagnostic specialties — are not less-precise versions of HR measurement. The *Machi*'s reading of *kultrun* drumming sequences during *machitun* ceremony, the *H'men*'s *sastun* (crystal) divination, the *Kallawaya*'s *coca quintu* reading, the *ayahuasquero*'s *icaros* sung to a specific patient, the *Midewiwin* fourth-degree healer's work with *Mide* scrolls — all of these are diagnostic and therapeutic practices in their own traditions. None of them are biomarker-visible by construction.

The panel is unanimous that Bios should not attempt to "integrate" any of these — not by claiming biomarker patterns predict divinatory outcomes, not by adding a "divination journal" surface that would trivialise the practice, and not by suggesting that biomarker readings can substitute for or validate a ceremonial diagnosis. Bios watches biomarkers; the practitioner practises. Both coexist in the owner's life, in the same way a stethoscope and a psychiatric interview coexist in biomedicine without one being reducible to the other.

What Bios *can* do is *not get in the way*. The push-side language in [AlertContentPolicy.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/AlertContentPolicy.kt) already sticks to factual data statements ("RHR +2σ for 48 h") rather than totalising interpretive ones. This is exactly the right register. The audit asks that it be preserved deliberately and that the localisation overlays not retreat from it.

### 3.3 Etiological pluralism — Bios's silence is correct

In many Americas traditions, illness etiology includes relational, ancestral, ceremonial, and spirit dimensions: Diné *Hózhǫ́* (the dynamic balance state, the disturbance of which underlies illness), Lakota *taku škaŋškaŋ* frames, Maya *ch'ulel* / soul-loss frames (related to but not identical with Mexican-American *susto*), Andean *pacha* relations between person and land, Amazonian *encanto* / spirit-of-place illness causation, the role of the dead and the unborn in Inuit etiology. Bios is silent on all of this. The panel's reading: this is the correct silence for an instrument whose epistemological scope is biomarker measurement. The instrument is the instrument; the practitioner is the practitioner; the owner moves between them.

What the panel asks for, as with the African audit, is *epistemological humility in the text*. The condition-pattern explanations should not foreclose other readings — they should report what Bios's biomarker observations show, and stop there. The current text largely does this; the manifesto principles that produce this restraint (Principle 6 "science-grounded, never fear-driven" plus Principle 7 "instrument, not coach") are well-aligned.

### 3.4 Historical depth deserves acknowledgement

The audit notes for the project's awareness: surgical traditions across the Indigenous Americas predate European contact by centuries. **Inca trepanation** is the best-documented Indigenous surgical tradition globally; thousands of trepanned skulls survive across Peru, with healing-rate evidence (osteoblastic remodelling at the trepanation site) indicating substantial survival rates — *higher* in the Inca-period sample than in 19th-century European trepanation. The Aztec ethnomedical literature is similarly deep: the **Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis** (Badianus Manuscript, 1552 — Martín de la Cruz, translated by Juan Badiano, both Indigenous Nahua physicians at the Colegio de Santa Cruz de Tlatelolco) is the oldest written American herbal, with 185 plant species illustrated and described in their medical use. The **Florentine Codex** (Sahagún, drawing on Nahua informants) Book 10 records the Nahua physician's role and the *tepahtiani* / *tlamatini* / *temiquitiani* differentiation. The **Codex Cruz-Badianus**, the **Relaciones Geográficas** medical sections, and the **Quechua ethnobotanical traditions** documented in 16th–17th century sources together constitute one of the deepest pre-modern medical literatures preserved anywhere.

The panel does not ask Bios to "capture" any of this. It asks that the project not frame Indigenous Americas medicine as ahistorical or as oral-only — both framings recur in popular accounts and both are wrong. The Codex Badianus is older than William Harvey's *De Motu Cordis*.

### 3.5 Federated learning and tribal-consent frameworks

The Bios roadmap mentions a federated-learning framework. The current state is no-federation-by-default. The panel asks specifically: *if and when* the federated-learning layer ships, the tribal-consent frameworks that govern collective Indigenous data must be navigated *before* enrolment of Indigenous-owner data into any federated pool. The relevant frameworks include:

- **Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board** — any research involving Diné community members or data sourced from Diné-resident devices requires NNHRRB review.
- **First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC) / OCAP®** — for First Nations data in Canada.
- **Native BioData Consortium protocols** for genomic and broader biodata.
- **Tribal IRBs** for the ~30 US tribes with their own IRBs, plus the IHS National IRB.
- **PAHO Indigenous-health frameworks** for federated work involving Latin American Indigenous communities.
- **CARE Principles compliance review** (per Carroll et al. 2020) as the broader frame above any specific institutional one.

The audit reiterates: *do not ship a federated layer that includes Indigenous-owner data without explicit tribal-consent navigation*. The not-by-default current posture is correct; the architecture should make tribal-consent opt-in surfaceable when the federated layer ships, not retrofit it after the fact.

### 3.6 Free for all — alignment with IHS and PAHO universal-coverage frames

Manifesto Principle 3's commitment to "no one should miss an early detection because they cannot afford a subscription" aligns with the IHS-eligibility frame (US — health services as treaty obligation to federally recognised tribes), with Indigenous Services Canada (federal obligation to First Nations / Inuit / Métis), and with the universal-coverage healthcare frames across most of Latin America (Mexico's *Seguro Popular* and successor programmes, Brazil's SUS, Argentina's universal-coverage system, the Bolivian SUS, etc.). A subscription-gated app would silently exclude exactly the populations these systems were established to serve. The panel notes the alignment is structural.

## 4. Latent strengths the codebase has not yet named

These are places where Bios is *already aligned* with Indigenous Americas concerns, and the audit recommends only that the alignment be named in documentation so it is preserved deliberately.

### 4.1 CARE / OCAP / Indigenous-data-sovereignty alignment is real and undocumented

The single most important under-documented strength. Bios's local-first SQLCipher storage, no-cloud-by-default architecture, no-Google-Play-Services dependency, encrypted-export-on-explicit-action sharing, differential-privacy-only-on-opt-in community layer, and refusal to monetise health data together constitute *exactly* the architectural commitments the CARE Principles (Carroll et al. 2020, *Sci Data*), OCAP® (FNIGC Canada), the Native BioData Consortium, and the Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board frameworks have been calling for over the last fifteen years. The project documentation frames these architectural choices in terms of individual privacy + LETHE threat model + post-Dobbs reproductive protection — which is correct but partial. Adding a paragraph in [PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md](../PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md) that explicitly names CARE / OCAP compatibility would close a documentation gap without requiring a code change, and would make Bios legible to Indigenous health-system partners (IHS, Indigenous Services Canada, PAHO Indigenous-health office, tribal health departments) without those partners having to do the translation work themselves.

The panel's strongest single recommendation in the audit is *not* a code change. It is a documentation change: name the alignment that already exists.

### 4.2 Manual / bone-setting traditions — analogous to African bonesetters

The Mexican-American *sobador/a* (manual therapy) and *huesero* (bone-setting) traditions, the Andean *jampi* / manual-therapy traditions, the Mapuche *ngutram*-adjacent manual work, and the various Mesoamerican manipulation traditions are analogues of the Yoruba bonesetter tradition the [African audit §4.4](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) discussed. Bios cannot reduce a fracture or perform a manipulation. The post-treatment monitoring window — distal-pulse / distal-warmth tracking, pain trajectory, asymmetry in HR and activity, swelling-related sleep disruption — is something Bios *could* monitor, with the same shape as the African audit's bonesetter recommendation. The `PAIN_SCORE` field at [MetricType.kt:79](../../android/bios-contracts/src/main/kotlin/com/bios/contracts/MetricType.kt#L79) is already in the schema. This is Tier-C work.

### 4.3 Midwifery / *partera* tradition and pregnancy-attendant gating

Same shape as [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV §2.6](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md). The *partera*-attended pregnancy is the dominant attendant frame in much of rural Mexico, Guatemala, Bolivia, Peru, parts of the US Mexican-American diaspora, and historically (and still) on many North American reservations. The `attendantType` field the African audit recommended (`BIOMEDICAL_OB`, `MIDWIFE_CERTIFIED`, `TRADITIONAL_BIRTH_ATTENDANT`, `OTHER_TRADITIONAL_PRACTITIONER`, `UNATTENDED`, `NOT_DISCLOSED`) serves the Americas catchment with one addition: `PARTERA` as a specific value, given the substantial documented institutional presence in Mexico and the Mexican Ministry of Health's formal recognition pathway for *parteras tradicionales*.

### 4.4 Variolation / inoculation precedent — Mesoamerican

Mesoamerican smallpox-inoculation practices are documented in the post-Conquest literature (Bernardino de Sahagún records Nahua practice) and predate Jennerian vaccination. Less internationally studied than the West African / Sudanese precedents the African audit noted, but the historical precedent exists. If/when Bios adds an `IMMUNIZATION` domain (per the [MEDICAL_PROFESSIONAL_POV §2.3](MEDICAL_PROFESSIONAL_POV.md) recommendation), the domain should accommodate non-WHO-standardised vaccine names and the Latin American Public Health vaccine schedules, which differ in several entries from the EU/US/UK schedules — particularly yellow fever (endemic in Amazonian / Cerrado / Chaco regions, in the routine schedule across much of South America, absent from EU/US/UK schedules).

### 4.5 Cold-weather / tropical-disease pattern overlap with African audit

The Inuit *Angakkuq*'s catchment is Arctic — frostbite, hypothermia, vitamin-D deficiency at extreme winter latitudes (the [vitamin-D-deficiency pattern](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) is partial coverage but specifically couples vitamin D to depression, not to bone health or immune function), polar-night circadian patterns where the existing 14-day baseline is wrong by construction. The Amazonian *vegetalista*'s catchment overlaps the African audit's tropical-disease pattern gap — malaria, dengue, chikungunya, leishmaniasis, Chagas disease, schistosomiasis — and the [AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV §2.1](AFRICAN_TRADITIONAL_POV.md) recommendation for a tropical-disease pattern overlay serves both catchments. The Mapuche *Machi*'s catchment is temperate / Mediterranean Chile and Argentina, mostly within the current pattern library's implicit range. The diversity of the Americas catchment spans every climate band Bios's current patterns implicitly do not address.

## 5. Recommendations, tiered

**Tier A — owner safety in Americas deployment, ship before any further pattern expansion**

1. **Ceremonial-context annotation + URGENT-tier gating during ceremony** (§2.1). Owner-set, time-bounded, manifesto-aligned. With the survival-threshold carve-out so Bios is not silent on a genuine cardiac event during ceremony. Single highest-priority gap for the North American panel; no analogue in prior audits.
2. **Altitude-aware SpO2 cutoffs** (§2.2). Same `EnvironmentalContext.elevationBand` annotation [SOWA_RIGPA_POV §2.1](SOWA_RIGPA_POV.md) recommends, serving the Andean *Kallawaya*, highland *curanderismo*, and US Southwest pueblo/Diné catchments simultaneously.
3. **`CEREMONIAL_CONTEXT` survival-threshold carve-out** (§2.1 detail) — even in active ceremony, push the alert when SpO2 <70 % sustained 10 min, HR >200 sustained 10 min, or other unambiguous survival-threshold readings. With ceremony-aware text ("alert your ceremony leader if present; emergency services if unsure").
4. **CARE / OCAP / Indigenous-data-sovereignty paragraph in [PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md](../PRIVACY_ARCHITECTURE.md)** (§4.1). Documentation-only, no code change. The single most leveraged change in the audit — names an alignment that already exists.

**Tier B — content and locale completeness for the Americas, next quarter**

5. **Latin America region configs in [RegionConfigProvider.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/config/RegionConfigProvider.kt)** (§2.3): MX, BR, AR, CL, PE, BO, CO, EC, GT, PY at minimum; `LATAM_DEFAULT` fallback. IHS-overlay and Indigenous Services Canada overlay as adjacent Tier-B work.
6. **Spanish-language localisation pipeline** (§2.4) — refactor [ConditionPatterns.kt](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/alerts/ConditionPatterns.kt) text fields to `strings.xml`, ship es-MX overlay first. Portuguese (pt-BR) second.
7. **Traditional pharmacopoeia annotation surface** (§2.7) — extend [MedicationAnnotationRepo](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/bios/app/data/MedicationAnnotationRepo.kt) to first-class herbal entries; pre-populate the reference list of ~25 most-physiologically-active Americas plants with known cardiovascular / endocrine / hepatic effects.
8. **Anatomy-question UI relabeling** (§2.6) — replace "Female-bodied / Male-bodied" with direct anatomy questions ("Have breast tissue?", "Have a cervix?", "Have prostate?"). Engineering: cosmetic. Cultural impact: substantial.
9. **IHS Adult Preventive Care Bundle as alternate screening catalog** (§2.5). Mexican IMSS as second alternate.

**Tier C — workflow and ecosystem fit, when the foundation is solid**

10. **Indigenous-language localisations** (§2.4): Quechua, Aymara, Guaraní, Diné Bizaad, Yucatec Maya, Nahuatl, Inuktitut, Cherokee, Lakota, Anishinaabemowin, Mapudungun — prioritised by speaker count and tradition-linguistic alignment.
11. **`PRACTITIONER_VISIT` rename** (§2.8) with `practitionerType` annotation; owner provides free-text role description for Indigenous-practitioner consultations.
12. **Print-ready practitioner share format** (§2.10) — A4 (Latin America, Canada) / US-Letter (IHS) single-page summary in device locale, with annotations as context bands. Pull-side, owner-triggered.
13. ***Partera*-attended pregnancy gating** (§4.3) with `PARTERA` as a specific `attendantType` value.
14. **Post-treatment manual-therapy monitoring template** (§4.2) for *sobador/a* / *huesero* / *jampi*-attended owners.
15. **Cultural-concepts-of-distress sensitivity in localisation** (§2.9) — Spanish, Quechua, Aymara, Nahuatl, Yucatec Maya, Diné Bizaad, Cherokee, Lakota overlays of `mental_health_correlate` suggested-action text should recommend "consult a practitioner you trust" rather than "consult a healthcare provider."

**Tier D — tribal-consent infrastructure (only when federated-learning layer activates)**

16. **Tribal-consent navigation framework** for federated-learning enrolment (§3.5). Do not ship a federated layer that includes Indigenous-owner data without this. NNHRRB / FNIGC / Tribal IRB / Native BioData Consortium / CARE Principles compliance review as the procedural floor.

**Do not adopt**

- A "ceremony detector" or any biomarker-based attempt to infer that the owner is in ceremony. Ceremonial context is owner-set or not present. The same logic that prevents Bios from inferring pregnancy from BBT prevents it from inferring sweat-lodge from HR.
- A "divinatory diagnosis" surface or any attempt to render the *Hataałii*'s Hand-trembling, the *H'men*'s *sastun*, the *Kallawaya*'s *coca quintu*, the *Machi*'s *kultrun* reading, or the *vegetalista*'s *icaros* in Bios's data model. These are not biomarker-visible by construction. Bios's silence on them is the correct silence.
- A "spirit-illness detector" or biomarker-based attempt to identify *susto*, *empacho*, *mal de ojo*, *bilis*, *nervios*, *ataque de nervios*, *mollera caída*, or any of the others. These are not biomarker-visible by construction. Same correct silence.
- A push-side "you should consult an Indigenous practitioner" recommendation. The owner is final; the practitioner is the owner's choice; Bios reports observations and lets the owner decide who to consult about them. This applies symmetrically to "you should see a doctor" — both belong only in pull-side or owner-triggered surfaces.
- A closed enum of Indigenous-practitioner role names maintained by Bios. The owner provides the role description as free-text. Bios does not maintain a taxonomy of Indigenous medical specialties; the catalogue in [MEDICAL_SPECIALTIES_WORLDWIDE.md §11](MEDICAL_SPECIALTIES_WORLDWIDE.md) is reference material, not data-model material.
- A federated-learning enrolment path for Indigenous-owner data without the tribal-consent framework in §3.5 / Tier D. The not-by-default current posture is correct; *do not retreat from it* under a "data is anonymised" justification that the CARE Principles explicitly anticipate and reject.
- A "validate vs. invalidate" stance on any tradition. Bios is an instrument. Instruments do not adjudicate epistemologies. The instrument reports what it sees and stops.

## 6. Summary line for the project

> Bios is, architecturally, the closest fit to Indigenous Americas data-sovereignty frameworks (CARE Principles, OCAP®, Native BioData Consortium protocols, Navajo Nation HRRB framework) of any consumer health product in its size class — built for a different stated reason and landing on the same shape. This alignment is real, genuinely strong, and currently un-named in the project documentation; the single highest-leverage change in the audit is a documentation paragraph that names it. At the content layer, Bios is implicitly mid-latitude temperate, implicitly sedentary, and implicitly biomedical-encounter-shaped: it has no model of ceremonial physiology (and the URGENT escalator will mis-fire during sweat lodge, sun dance, vision quest, peyote ceremony, and ayahuasca ceremony — eroding alert credibility precisely in populations most likely to participate); no altitude modelling (the Andean *Kallawaya* catchment is in the same altitude regime as Lhasa and shares the same fix the Sowa Rigpa audit recommends); no region configs south of the US; no Spanish, Portuguese, Quechua, Aymara, Guaraní, Diné Bizaad, Yucatec Maya, Nahuatl, Inuktitut, Cherokee, or Mapudungun localisation; no IHS or PAHO screening cadence; and a `Female-bodied / Male-bodied` UI binary that does not accommodate two-spirit, *nádleehi*, *winkte*, *lhamana*, or the third-and-fourth-gender categories many nations recognise. None of the gaps require the manifesto to retreat. The orthogonality between biomarker observation and divinatory / ceremonial diagnosis (*ndilniihii*, *sastun*, *coca quintu*, *kultrun* reading, *icaros*) is correct silence and should be preserved deliberately. The audit's combined Tier-A recommendation: ceremonial-context annotation + altitude annotation + the CARE/OCAP documentation paragraph, shipped together as the "Bios in the Americas, on Indigenous terms" capability that names the alignment that already exists and closes the safety gaps that do not.
