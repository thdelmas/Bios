# Medical Specialties — Worldwide Catalogue

**Scope:** A reference list of medical specialties recognised across the world's major medical traditions — Western biomedicine, the great codified non-Western systems (TCM, Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa, Kampo, Korean), and the regional / indigenous traditions that remain in living practice.
**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Branch:** `feat/metric-info-sheets-on-read`
**Purpose:** A neutral inventory for downstream design work (regional content, owner self-description, integration with non-biomedical practitioners). Not an endorsement of any tradition's claims. Bios remains an instrument, not a coach — see [MANIFESTO.md](../../MANIFESTO.md).
**Auditor:** Claude (Opus 4.7)

This document does **not** rank, validate, or compare clinical efficacy across traditions. Inclusion is descriptive: if a tradition formally trains and licenses (or in living practice recognises) a specialty, it appears here.

## 2. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM, 中医)

## 3. Ayurveda (India, Sri Lanka, Nepal)

Ayurveda is regulated in India by the Ministry of AYUSH; recognised as a national medical system. The classical "Ashtanga" eight-limb division dates to the *Sushruta Samhita* and *Charaka Samhita*.

### 3.1 Classical Ashtanga Ayurveda (eight branches)
- Kayachikitsa — internal medicine
- Shalya tantra — surgery
- Shalakya tantra — ENT, ophthalmology, dentistry, head and neck
- Kaumarabhritya — paediatrics, obstetrics, neonatology
- Agada tantra — toxicology, forensic
- Bhuta vidya — psychiatry / psychosomatic ("spirit-possession" in classical terms)
- Rasayana — rejuvenation, geriatrics, longevity
- Vajikarana — reproductive and sexual medicine, fertility

### 3.2 Modern Ayurvedic specialties (BAMS / MD-Ayurveda track)
- Panchakarma — five-action detoxification therapeutics
- Dravyaguna — materia medica / pharmacology
- Rasashastra — alchemical / mineral pharmacy
- Bhaishajya kalpana — pharmaceutics
- Roga nidana — pathology and diagnosis
- Swasthavritta — preventive medicine and public health
- Prasuti tantra — obstetrics and gynaecology
- Striroga — women's diseases
- Manasa roga — mental health
- Marma chikitsa — vital-point therapy
- Nadi pariksha — pulse diagnosis
- Yoga therapy (often co-licensed under AYUSH)

## 4. Unani Tibb (Greco-Arabic / Islamic medicine)

Codified from Hippocratic-Galenic humoral theory by Ibn Sina, Al-Razi, Al-Zahrawi. State-recognised in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka; living practice in Iran, Afghanistan, Central Asia, parts of the Levant.

- Moalajat — internal medicine
- Jarahat — surgery
- Ilmul Qabalat wa Amraz e Niswan — obstetrics and gynaecology
- Amraze Atfal — paediatrics
- Ain, Uzn, Anaf, Halaq wa Asnan — ophthalmology, ENT, dental
- Amraze Jild wa Zohrawiya — dermatology and venereology
- Ilmul Advia — pharmacology, materia medica
- Saidla — pharmacy
- Tashreehul Badan — anatomy
- Munafeul Aza — physiology (humoral)
- Kulliyat — fundamentals / theory
- Ilaj bil Tadbeer — regimenal therapy (cupping/hijama, leeching, massage, exercise, bath, fasting)
- Ilmul Saidla wa Murakkabat — compound formulations
- Mahiyatul Amraz — pathology
- Tahaffuzi wa Samaji Tibb — preventive and social medicine

## 5. Siddha medicine (Tamil)

Living tradition centred in Tamil Nadu and the Tamil diaspora; regulated under AYUSH. Attributed to the 18 Siddhars; alchemical / mineral pharmacy is more central than in Ayurveda.

- Pothu Maruthuvam — general medicine
- Sirappu Maruthuvam — special / advanced internal medicine
- Kuzhanthai Maruthuvam — paediatrics
- Magalir Maruthuvam — women's health, obstetrics, gynaecology
- Noi Naadal — diagnosis (eight-fold examination, including pulse, urine, voice, tongue, eye, touch, complexion, stool)
- Gunapadam — materia medica (plant, mineral, animal)
- Nanju Noolum Maruthuva Neethi Noolum — toxicology and medical jurisprudence
- Varmam — vital-point therapy and martial-medical art
- Thokkanam — manipulation / massage therapy
- Yoga Maruthuvam — yoga therapy
- Aruvai Maruthuvam — surgery (limited)
- Kayakarpam — rejuvenation, longevity
- Muppu — alchemical "universal salt" preparations

## 6. Sowa Rigpa (Tibetan medicine)

State-recognised in India (Sikkim, Ladakh, Himachal, Arunachal, Darjeeling) under AYUSH; living practice in Tibet, Bhutan, Mongolia, Nepal, Buryatia, Tuva, Kalmykia. Codified in the *Gyud Zhi* (Four Tantras).

- Nyepa diagnosis (rLung / mKhris-pa / Bad-kan — three humours)
- Internal medicine
- Paediatrics
- Gynaecology
- Geriatrics / Rejuvenation (bcud-len)
- Wound and surgical practice (historically; now limited)
- Toxicology
- Mental and "spirit" disorders
- Pulse diagnosis
- Urine diagnosis
- Moxibustion (me btsa')
- Golden-needle therapy (gser khab)
- Bloodletting (gtar ga)
- Massage (bsku mnye)
- Mineral and precious-pill pharmacy (rin chen ril bu)
- Behavioural and dietary regulation

## 7. Traditional Korean Medicine (한의학, Hanui-hak)

Licensed in South Korea as a parallel medical system (KMD vs MD). Curriculum at Korean Medicine colleges (6-year), specialties under the Association of Korean Medicine.

- Internal medicine (한방내과) — further split by organ system: cardiopulmonary, hepato-digestive, renal, neuropsychiatric
- Gynaecology (한방부인과)
- Paediatrics (한방소아과)
- Neuropsychiatry (한방신경정신과)
- Acupuncture and moxibustion (침구과)
- Rehabilitation medicine (한방재활의학과)
- Ophthalmology, otorhinolaryngology, dermatology (안이비인후피부과)
- Sasang constitutional medicine (사상체질의학) — four-type constitutional system, distinctive to Korea
- Saam acupuncture
- Herbal pharmacy (본초학)
- Preventive medicine (예방의학)
- Diagnostic methods (진단학)

## 8. Kampo (Japanese herbal medicine, 漢方)

Japanese adaptation of classical Chinese formulae; integrated into Japanese biomedical practice — over 90% of Japanese MDs prescribe Kampo formulas, and 148 formulations are covered by Japanese national health insurance.

- Kampo internal medicine
- Kampo gynaecology
- Kampo paediatrics
- Kampo gastroenterology (a particularly strong domain)
- Kampo psychosomatic medicine
- Kampo geriatrics
- Sho diagnosis (証) — pattern identification
- Abdominal diagnosis (腹診, fukushin) — distinctive Japanese contribution
- Pulse diagnosis (脈診, myakushin)
- Acupuncture and moxibustion (鍼灸) — licensed separately as hari-kyu-shi
- Anma / Shiatsu — manual therapy (separate licensing)
- Judo-seifuku (柔道整復) — bone-setting / manual reduction, licensed paramedical

## 9. Other codified Asian systems

- **Mongolian traditional medicine** — closely related to Sowa Rigpa; specialties include internal medicine, bone-setting (bariach), bloodletting, herbal pharmacy, moxibustion, manual therapy (chimedeg)
- **Vietnamese traditional medicine (Đông y / Thuốc Nam)** — Northern school (Sino-derived) and Southern school (indigenous herbs); internal medicine, gynaecology, paediatrics, acupuncture, cupping (giác hơi), bone-setting
- **Thai traditional medicine** — recognised under the Department for Development of Thai Traditional and Alternative Medicine; pharmacy (peht phaen boran), midwifery (phadung khan), massage (Nuad Thai — UNESCO intangible heritage), spiritual medicine (saiyasart)
- **Khmer traditional medicine (Cambodia)** — kru khmer practitioners; herbal, manual, ritual
- **Lao traditional medicine** — herbal and bone-setting traditions
- **Burmese traditional medicine** — Department of Traditional Medicine; Desana (theory), Bethitza (purification), Netkhatta (astrological), Vijjadhara (alchemical) — four classical branches
- **Jamu (Indonesia)** — herbal medicine, recognised, regulated by BPOM; midwifery (dukun bayi), massage (pijat), bone-setting (sangkal putung)
- **Hilot (Philippines)** — manual therapy, bone-setting, midwifery (komadrona), pulse diagnosis (pulso)

## 10. African medical traditions

African traditional medicine is recognised by the African Union and WHO AFRO; codification varies by country (formalised in South Africa via the Traditional Health Practitioners Act 2007, Ghana via TMPC, Nigeria, Zimbabwe, Ethiopia). Roles overlap with anthropological categories.

- Herbalist (general practitioner — e.g. *inyanga* Zulu, *dibia* Igbo, *babalawo* Yoruba diagnostic, *medhanit awaki* Amharic)
- Bone-setter (e.g. *Yoruba bone-setters* — internationally studied)
- Traditional birth attendant / Midwife
- Diviner (e.g. *sangoma* Nguni, *nganga* Bantu, *babalawo* Yoruba Ifá divination)
- Spirit / Ancestral practitioner
- Surgical traditions — historically including caesarean section in Buganda, trepanation in Tanzania/Kenya, vaccination (variolation) across the Sahel
- Snake-bite specialist
- Mental and behavioural specialist
- Children's specialist (paediatric herbalist)
- Ritual purification specialist
- Ethiopian / Eritrean traditional bloodletter (*wugat*)
- Maghrebi *tabib* — Greco-Arabic-influenced herbalist tradition
- Ethiopian church-medicine traditions (Tewahedo)

## 12. Other historically codified systems

- **Tibb-i Yunani in the Ottoman tradition** — closely related to Unani; survived into the late 19th century in Anatolia
- **Persian Medicine (Tibb-e Sonnati)** — distinct from Unani in Iran, now revitalised in Iranian universities with MD-PhD programmes
- **Mesopotamian / Babylonian medicine** — historical, but influences modern Iraqi folk practice
- **Egyptian traditional medicine** — historical and folk continuation
- **European folk and monastic medicine** — Hildegardian medicine (Germanic monastic), Scandinavian rune-staff medicine, Slavic znakhar, Romani folk medicine
- **Hippocratic-Galenic humoralism** — historical root of Western, Unani, and Persian medicine

## 15. Notes on the catalogue

1. **Recognition varies.** A "specialty" here means a discipline that is either (a) formally licensed by a national health authority, (b) recognised under WHO ICD-11 Chapter 26 (Traditional Medicine Module), or (c) demonstrably codified and in continuous living practice with documented diagnostic and therapeutic methods.
2. **The list is not exhaustive.** Every region has folk specialties that have never been catalogued in writing. The absence of a tradition is silence, not negation.
3. **Bios's posture.** Bios neither validates nor invalidates any tradition's claims. If a future surface asks the owner "who do you consult about your health," the answer space should be wide enough to include all of these. The owner is final.
4. **Anti-cultural-erasure stance.** Naming non-Western specialties using their own terms (Kayachikitsa, not "Ayurvedic internal medicine"; Hataalii, not "Navajo shaman") is the minimum respect any catalogue of this kind owes the traditions it lists.
